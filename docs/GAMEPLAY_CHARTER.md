# THE THULANS — GAMEPLAY CHARTER
*Survival / moral-management. One Hearth. One long winter of arithmetic. Who endures?*

> **Governing question (RULING 019):** who gets to decide what another human being must endure.
> **Governing rule (RULING 001, the Intimacy Rule):** never let the Mountain become bigger than the people crushed beneath it.

---

## 1. Design thesis

The player is **not** the hero. The player is the **Hearth Steward** — the local administrator who allocates air, water, heat, food, and work, and who signs the lists of who receives and who is spent. Every mechanic converts a *person* into a *decision*.

The game is a survival loop where the resources are real and the moral premise is manufactured — mirroring the fiction's core: **the scarcity is real; the causal story is fraudulent.** The player will discover, mid-game, that some shortages were staged, and must decide what to do with that truth.

**One-line pitch:** *Frostpunk's pressure with This War of Mine's people, where the "miracle" that saves your district was a reserve the state already found — and proving it may kill everyone.*

---

## 2. Non-negotiable canon rules (the tone bible)

1. **Material causes only.** No magic, no Void, no cosmic entity, no gravity negation (RULINGS 005, 024, 030). "Graviton" is drift slang, never a mechanic.
2. **Varek is a legend, not the player.** He is a rare world-event that disrupts the system; the player is an ordinary functionary.
3. **No clean revolution, no restoration.** The macro ending can be catastrophic; the micro ending must contain life (RULING 013).
4. **The state is not cartoon evil.** The Council's arithmetic is defensible; that is what makes it monstrous.
5. **Consent is manufactured, not forced.** The system makes honorable people volunteer. The player's job is often to *accept* that offer.
6. **Audience gate (RULING 014).** Tier A names in UI; Tier B as texture only. Six-name ceiling for any onboarding.

---

## 3. Player role

**The Hearth Steward** of a lower district (default: a Hearth like Azg-Haíms). You report up through the Ministry of Continuity to the Council of Three.

- You do **not** set the quota; you meet it.
- You do **not** invent the Tithe; you **supply names** to the Wahlja.
- You **can** investigate survey dates, hide evidence, protect individuals, falsify ledgers, or expose the mechanism — at escalating risk.

The player is complicit by design. The game's horror is that you are not a villain — you are doing the arithmetic.

---

## 4. Core loop (one "shift cycle")

```
ASSESS district state ─► ALLOCATE scarce resources ─► ASSIGN labor & risk
        ▲                                                      │
        │                                                      ▼
   CONSEQUENCES  ◄── EVENT (fault shear, aquifer, fever, unrest) ◄── MEET QUOTA?
        │
        ▼
   RECORD (ledger, faith, evidence, grief) ─► next cycle
```

Each cycle: shortages bite, people ask, the Council demands, and the Tithe option appears as the "cheap" solution. The player is always choosing **who pays**.

---

## 5. Systems

| System | Mechanic | Thematic payload |
|---|---|---|
| **Resources** | Air, Water, Heat, Food, Ore, Marks | Scarcity is real; hoarding is punished |
| **The Vertical Economy** | Ladder: labor → entitlement → body → life | You can convert a person *down* the ladder to buy survival |
| **Entitlements** | Having Marks ≠ having permission | Control over allocation = control over behavior |
| **Marrow contracts** | Sell body for provisions; degrades the worker | The poverty loop; "No Hearth prices blood" |
| **Quotas** | Council target per cycle; failure docks rations | The state's clock vs the district's people |
| **Faith & Legitimacy** | Belief meter; Reveal Rites restore it | Manufactured divine causality |
| **Order** | Enforcers, strikes, dissent | Fear holds the system as much as faith |
| **Ark clock** | Hidden berth allocation for the elite | The clause the defense never states |
| **Truth/Evidence** | Survey dates, ledgers, testimony | The IP's signature: "When was it surveyed?" |

**Population are named individuals**, not counters — each with a need, a skill, and a name you will have to write on a list.

---

## 6. The moral engine: the Reveal Rite

The central system. When scarcity worsens, the state offers a **miracle**: a reserve will be "revealed" if the district performs a **Soul-Tenth** (Life Tithe).

- The reserve was **pre-surveyed** — the state already knew.
- The player may **accept** (district survives, a named person dies, faith rises), **refuse** (quota fails, unrest rises), or **investigate** (learn the date; risk exposure).
- The Wahlja supplies candidate names: volunteers, debtors, dissidents, knowledge risks. The player may **protect** someone — at the cost of someone else.

**The crime is the timing, not the sacrifice.** The game must let the player feel the temptation to do the math the state wants.

---

## 7. Evidence & truth as a resource

- The player accumulates **Evidence** (survey dates, dispatch ribbons, ledger mismatches).
- **Expose** it → legitimacy collapses, slow decline becomes immediate collapse (RULING 013); you may save a few, lose many.
- **Conceal** it → the district endures a while longer on a lie you now know is a lie.
- **Partial disclosure** → recruit allies, risk suppression.
- The endgame question is the film's: *did you have the right to tell them without knowing what would happen next?*

This is the IP's thesis expressed as a resource-management dilemma — and it is the game's unique hook.

---

## 8. Win / lose / end states

There is **no "win the Mountain."** Possible endings (all carry cost):
1. **Managed Lie** — you keep the Hearth breathing on concealed truth. Survival, moral erosion.
2. **Exposure & Collapse** — truth spreads; order fails; you save a few (the micro must contain life).
3. **The Berth** — you secure an ark berth for your people; 6 of 7 are left behind.
4. **The Hearth Endures** — smallest ending: you lose the district's output but keep a core alive by truthful obligation between people, not manufactured necessity.

The "good" outcome is the *smallest* one — consistent with RULING 013.

---

## 9. Tone, content & intimacy rules

- Tactile industrial: iron, oil, wet wool, coal smoke. No clean sci-fi surfaces.
- Quiet and granular over spectacle; the horror is administrative.
- Named losses, funerals, wiped slates — grief must be specific.
- No gore for its own sake; consequence, not shock.
- The Intimacy Rule governs every system: if it doesn't touch a person, cut it.

## 11. Platform & scope tiers

- **Target:** PC (Steam), single-player, mouse/UI-driven; controller optional.
- **Prototype (gate 1):** one Hearth, one cycle, three resources, one Tithe decision. Prove the emotional hook.
- **Vertical slice (gate 2):** full Vertical Economy, 8–12 cycles, one Reveal Rite arc, 2 endings.
- **Full (gate 3):** multiple Hearths/tiers, Varek world-event, ark clock, 4 endings, evidence system.

Do **not** build the Mountain before the Hearth is crushing.

---

## 12. Production gates (evidence-first)

Per the working standard: every systems claim is measured, not asserted.
- Simulation tests (resource balance, death spiral, no-unwinnable-softlock) as executable assertions.
- Balance changes bound to a hash and a replay; no hand-waved tuning.
- Narrative claims cite `RULINGS.md`; no claim-drift.
- Evidence JSON for each gate: `evidence_status: measured`, `status: PENDING_HUMAN_APPROVAL`.
