## The locked visual read

A hulking, hunched, deep-sea-diving-suit-like **penal industrial breaker**: massive dented shoulder housings rising over a small low head; a crudely riveted sheet-iron mask **fitted over an actual living face at eye level**, single narrow slit with an eye behind it; a huge patched rear boiler/pump assembly with accessible shutoff, vent stacks, cam and chain; a **single oversized powered breaker bar** to one side with obvious driven linkage; giant uneven work boots and frame legs capable of moving along mine track; an **exhausted, conscious human** visibly caught behind chest bars and limb restraints, with one human hand hanging beneath its cuff. The iron is rough, maintained badly, and much larger than the person. The machine has the agency, not the prisoner.

- **Body:** gaunt but alive, human skin and worn cloth; not a corpse/ghoul, no gruesome anatomy exhibition.
- **Head:** the eye slit is **not an empty box above the skull**. Mask and iron collar are over the face and can be dismantled with tools.
- **Feeding:** one **thin flexible line** enters near the mouth. It is not a breathing hose or pressure pipe. Steam/power hoses belong to the apparatus, **not surgically inserted into the torso**.
- **Chest:** an industrial serial/inventory plate is appropriate. The image's **"9" is a placeholder** for this breaker unit; it must **not** identify Ansen as Nessa's separate **Frame 9**. Ansen's precise serial is **not locked**. Art may replace the number without invalidating the chassis lock.
- **Mechanical design:** structural rails carry its own mass and tool reactions; prisoner cuffs compel unwanted motion. The prisoner is not an able pilot, and the work bar does not depend on their voluntary grip.
- **Finish:** ugly sheet iron, crude repairs, weld beads, soot and oil at their actual sources; the image is a **form/materials reference**, not a final inked graphic-novel interior or cover.

## Chapter Six rescue — now revised in the reading draft

The breaker still advances, drags a boot, cycles while the prisoner cannot stop it and threatens Varek with its huge powered bar. Varek **shears the exposed drive pin**, dropping the breaker; the back cam momentarily continues to turn without load. He steadies the heavy chest rail, **closes the steam feed and vents pressure**. The ensuing fight, second rig shutdown, Odo, Bram, damaged Varek pressure line, and human continuity remain unchanged.

**After the danger clears**, the first prisoner is still physically fastened inside the stopped iron. **Varek grips and bends the front chest crossbar outward**, breaking its fastenings without crushing the prisoner, and holds the cage open. **Kaelen** uses a knife/chisel to cut inner bindings, free the injured wrist, remove the riveted mask, and unclasp/withdraw the feeding line. **Varek lifts the living boy out** and lowers him to the ground; Kaelen supports the injured wrist. Varek then helps free the second miner. This makes visible the difference between stopping a machine and freeing the human trapped in it.

**Story boundary:** This remains the same first broken-wrist prisoner identified as Ansen under RULING 035. The two miners survive; Ansen's established later appearances, Nessa's separate captivity and the LABORA ending are unchanged.
