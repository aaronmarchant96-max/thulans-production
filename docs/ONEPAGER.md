# THE THULANS
*A post-human rescue saga about who gets to decide what another human being must endure.*

**Logline (long):**
Centuries after his civilization is gone, an engineered rescue giant awakens inside a
dying mountain city whose rulers have turned scarcity into religion. When he discovers
that workers have been selling labor, possessions, marrow and finally their lives for
access to dwindling resources officials had already surveyed and deliberately withheld,
exposing the truth forces him to choose between destroying a murderous lie and
destroying the system millions still depend on.

**Logline (short):**
A post-human rescuer awakens centuries late to find that his people's ethic of
voluntary sacrifice has become a machine for manufacturing consent.

**Thematic backbone:** A civilization can survive the exhaustion of a mountain. What it
cannot survive indefinitely is turning every form of love and sacrifice into a pricing
mechanism.

**The setting's central distinction: the scarcity is real. The causal story is
fraudulent.**

---

## Varek
Eight feet of permanently sealed cast iron, hydraulics, and riveted boiler-plate — an
engineered structural rescue anchor built by a vanished civilization. He wakes believing
something of his home survived; what he finds is that his people survived biologically
while their moral world died.

> His language survived. Its moral grammar was inverted.

His story is personal before it is political: he stops trying to recover his lost
civilization and begins protecting the actual people standing in front of him.

> "I had a mother."

## The Mountain
A continent-sized sacred industrial fortress — the vertical world of the Thulans. From
the high bastions of Garuni to the soot-tier working levels of Azg-Haíms, across a
2,000-meter industrial chasm.

## The Vertical Economy
Hearth Marks. Provision entitlements. Marrow contracts. The Life Tithe. A civilization
that learned to price everything a person can give — up to and including the self —
while calling the last step sacred.

> "A body may be given. It should not have to be sold."

## Wulfila's Tithe
A sincere volunteer whose district walked the whole ladder — overtime, tools, marks,
entitlements, debt, marrow — before the state demanded the last thing. He took the logic
seriously. He offered his life. The water existed — the aquifer had been surveyed and
its release deliberately delayed.

## The Reveal
When Wulfila's death is exposed as manufactured, the truth travels through surveyors,
shift boards, manifests, guild presses, and ordinary logistics. Over weeks, not days,
it destroys the legitimacy of state and religion — and slow decline becomes immediate
collapse.

## The Collapse Dilemma
Varek was right about the lie and wrong about the cost of exposing it. The lie was
monstrous. The lie was also load-bearing. He gave people freedom without giving them a
way to survive it.

## The Ending Philosophy
No restoration. No clean revolution. The macro ending is catastrophic; the micro ending
contains life. Truthful obligation returns to the Hearth. Varek's final voluntary
deconstruction and martyrdom is captured in the canonical short story:
`docs/THE_DISMANTLING_OF_VAREK.md`.

> "Will the Mountain protect us?"
> "No."
> "Then who will?"
> "We will."
