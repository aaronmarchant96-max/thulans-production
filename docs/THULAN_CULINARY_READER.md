# Thulan Food and Domestic Life
*Production Reference: Kitchens, Rations, and Dialect*

---

### Core Practice

Food in the mountain is defined by strict technical preservation applied to poor ingredients. 

Because grain flour, fresh meat, and clean water are rationed, lower-drift households rely on long processing times rather than expensive raw materials:
* **The Slate:** Food is served on cleaved roofing slate rather than crockery. The slate holds heat from the stove, doesn't break when dropped on rock, and is easy to scrape clean. A wiped rim is standard practice; leaving grease on the edge of the plate is considered slovenly, even in a flooded drift.
* **The Stock Pot:** No scraps are discarded. Bones, leek tops, and crayfish shells are boiled continuously for two days, strained through charcoal and cloth, and kept at a bare simmer on banked coals.
* **The Divide:** State rations provide gray nutrient cakes and flour vouchers; the household supplies the labor to make them edible through vinegar-pickling, smoking over timber shavings, and garlic pastes.

### Pantry by Zone

| Zone | Primary Ingredients | Processing Method | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lower Drifts (Tiers 7–8)** | Bracket fungus, wood grubs, cave eels, ditch crayfish, water-rats | Long boiling, heavy vinegar pickling, cold smoke over mine timber | Strong earthy, sulfurous, and allium flavors. High reliance on saltpeter cures. |
| **Mid-Drifts & Lamp Terraces (Tiers 4–6)** | Wild rock leeks, turnip paste, watercress, bitter radishes | Fermentation in crocks, steaming over boiler exhaust | Provides essential greens and sharp acid to cut through heavy grease. |
| **Aquifer Basins** | Cave trout, freshwater snails, brine shrimp, mineral spring water | Quick steaming, salt-curing roe, clean unsmoked broths | The cleanest, lightest cooking in the mountain; relies on fresh water rather than heavy smoke. |
| **Summit Terraces (Tier 0)** | Sheep tallow, dried goat curd, wind-dried mutton jerky, juniper needles | Wind drying, rendering in iron cauldrons | Calorie-dense food suited to sub-zero drafts and thin air. |
| **Upper Spire (Tiers 1–2)** | Hydroponic barley flour, dried orchard pears, cured lamb, honey | Baking, braising, pastry work | Mild, clean foods eaten by administrative cadres. Little smoke or vinegar. |

### Standard Techniques

1. **Clarified Broth (*Svart-Wato*):** A forty-eight-hour reduction of roasted bones and charred leek roots, clarified through charcoal dust and linen. Dark, viscous with gelatin, and served hot in tin or bronze mugs before meals.
2. **Pressed Terrine (*Thul-Fisk*):** Flaked cave fish, braised rodent meat, and blanched chives packed into iron pans with cold mutton tallow or gelatin. Sliced cold with draw-knives into firm rectangles.
3. **Steam Dumplings (*Klumpôs*):** Coarse beetle-flour dough stuffed with chopped fungus, cured fat, and wild chives. Steamed over boiler vents or seared on hot shovel blades.
4. **Trench Garum (*Garn-Sós*):** Fermented fish viscera, eel trimmings, and salted crickets aged six months in stone jars. Used as a salty, pungent seasoning across all lower-tier stews.
5. **Vinegar Pickles (*Apeit-Syre*):** Sour beet and leek vinegars used to pickle quail eggs, bitter radishes, and mushroom caps to prevent scurvy.
6. **Timber Smoking (*Smók-Flesk*):** Cold-smoking salted meats over scrap larch shavings and dried shelf fungus to prevent fly strike and rot in humid air.
7. **Paper Pastry (*Pást-Haíms*):** Flour dough rolled paper-thin, wrapped around smoked eel, leeks, and black lentils, and baked on iron hearth stones. Traditional food given to young miners on their first shift.
8. **Marrow Mash (*Grout-Faírg*):** Mashed roasted turnips and lentil meal whipped with hot bone tallow and served in a neat mound on warm slate with a spoonful of fermented green leek oil.

### Dialects and Vocabulary

Language in the mountain breaks into three working registers:

* **Foundry Inscriptions (Old Latinate):** Archaic stamping found on three-hundred-year-old structural beams and Varek's chassis plates (`ORA ET LABORA`, `SOLI DEO GLORIA`). Known mostly to master tool-wrights and senior surveyors.
* **Registry Speech:** The dry, administrative terminology of the Ministry of Continuity. Used in shift manifests, dispatch ribbons, and court orders (`fathom-tare`, `shear probability`, `casualty allocation`, `life-entitlement`).
* **Hearth Speech:** The colloquial Germanic dialect spoken in domestic quarters, kitchens, and drift families. Used for food, tools, lullabies, and funeral rites (`Swin-Flesk` for pork/rat, `Blôþ-Mats` for blood cake, `Mido-Stáin` for pine-needle mead, `Katils` for the communal kettle).
