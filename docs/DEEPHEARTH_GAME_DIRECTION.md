# Thulans: Deephearth — Game Direction

Status: approved direction and explicitly marked proposals, 2026-09-18. This document governs the proposed game, not the separate Varek literary/cinematic continuity.

## Approved game direction

- **Genre and player role:** A colony-management simulation. The colony and its people are the subject; no fixed individual protagonist, mandatory Wulfila storyline, or character-controlled adventure is required. Residents can become central through emergent events. A player-created resident remains an optional, unapproved feature.
- **Visual target:** The user-approved Deephearth UI concept image from the September 18 conversation is the art-direction reference. Detailed 2D pixel art, readable rooms and expressive small residents in the visual vein of Stardew Valley and Dave the Diver; Gothic stone architecture, dwarven mining, soot, brass, hydraulics, steam, furnaces and dieselpunk industrial machinery. RimWorld is a reference for colony controls, not the art style. The image is a concept, not an implemented game screen or ready-made asset sheet.
- **Interaction:** Colony control throughout. No required player-avatar movement or separate Dave the Diver-style mining minigame. Mining, hauling, machinery and resident activity belong to the colony simulation.
- **World:** The game takes place in the Thulan mountain civilization. Preserve established world foundations unless a specific game ruling changes them. Vertical districts and the polluted surface are potential spaces for expansion; a mandatory linear climb to the summit has NOT been approved.
- **Narrative:** The original Varek short story establishes tone and explores one possible tragic arc; it is not the required plot or ending of every Deephearth campaign. No fixed game protagonist or complete authored campaign has been approved. Emergent resident histories and consequential colony decisions are the intended focus.

## Visual production target

Create a functioning colony-management game whose live play retains the approved concept's layered underground rooms, tiny workers, warm furnace glow against cold blue-grey stone, readable machinery, and integrated resource, resident and event panels. Preserve readability and performance when translating the concept into consistent tile sets, sprites, animation, lighting and UI. Exact tile dimensions, camera angle, resolution and asset pipeline remain prototype decisions, not locked canon.

## Proposed first playable vertical slice — NOT implemented

One Lower Drift hearth; approximately six residents; heat, water and food; basic jobs and movement; one machinery failure; selectable resident and work controls; one consequential Event Forge dilemma with persistent results. These are prototype targets, not claims about existing code or tested features.

## Narrative boundaries

- Most Thulans are not assumed to be lifelong underground captives. Some exceptionally isolated or debt-trapped residents may never have seen the sky; population percentages and biological adaptations are not established.
- The surface may be a polluted, damaged industrial landscape under a bruised horizon, with rare surviving vegetation. Its precise geography and mechanics remain to be developed.
- Bram, Sanna and Wulfila may inform examples and authored material; their presence, ages, relationships and outcomes are not mandatory across procedurally generated colonies. Do not silently promote details from a concept story into universal game facts.
- The game's story should not require a happy ending, a predetermined collapse, or a forced sacrifice. Consequences must follow actual game state and player decisions.

## Reference roles, not copied designs

- RimWorld: colony control, work priorities, autonomous residents and emergent events.
- Stardew Valley: pixel-art readability, character expression and intimate environments.
- Dave the Diver: rich pixel-art atmosphere, lighting and animation, **not** its player-controlled gameplay loop.
- Dwarf Fortress: mining, multi-level settlement and emergent systems, **not** its learning curve as a design target.

## Decision hygiene

Approved direction is not proof of implementation. Distinguish LOCKED WORLD CANON, APPROVED GAME DIRECTION, PROPOSED FEATURE, and VERIFIED IMPLEMENTATION in all new documents. Record changes rather than retroactively rewriting historical approvals.
