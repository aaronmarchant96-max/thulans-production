## Story

**Childhood.** Ansen and Nessa are unrelated orphans who grew up together in Fairguni's service passages. Around age thirteen, they share stolen hot bread, compete over portions ("half each"), bicker over an oversized coat and imagine what a future with an oven might be like. Give their ordinary life actual space and humour. They are not romantic children. Their bread theft earns each a **three-month** sentence in separate Welded frames. After **five years**, the release has never come.

**Nessa's work.** The hauling frame at the transfer gallery imposes powered movement on her conscious body. She braces before a stroke she recognizes by its sound; worn feed-line equipment, shoulder pressure injuries and involuntary motion make the body horror visible. She knows the working rhythm. **Pell**, a younger worker, responds to two short nasal hums from her, preventing an injury during ordinary work. **Edda**, an experienced rigger, adjusts padding or checks her feed line because the station cannot afford an injured operator. Edda knows the prisoner only as *Nine*. They are difficult, practical colleagues, not saints and beneficiaries placed there to force sentiment.

**Varek's rescue.** Ansen is the first broken-wrist miner Varek and Kaelen free in **Chapter Six**. The other miner freed then is not Nessa. Ansen remains present to help in **Chapter Ten**, and his freedom is confirmed in **Chapter Eleven**. Only **after** those established appearances does he search for Nessa. There is time for **one spoken lead**, not a written-record investigation: an old intake assignment named the breaker and transfer-haul destinations, and in the wash passage he hears the transfer hauls still run. The exact six-hour evacuation clock and route require verification.

**Reunion.** Ansen finds Nessa still in Frame 9. They don't deliver speeches about five years or love. She notices his hair; he sees the injured wrist and the frame. Possible **candidate dialogue**, not a lock: "Your hair." / "What about it?" / "It's terrible." The childhood familiarity returns awkwardly. A small new closeness between adults is possible but neither names it. The tube and restraint design still limit how much she can speak. Do not pile the coat, bread and cold-feet callbacks into one scene.

**Their joint success.** A rushed shutdown threatens to close a drop shutter across the only route for Edda and Pell. Edda is injured **in the same gallery established by Nessa's earlier work**; Pell stays to help her. Nessa knows a catch is out of alignment. Ansen wants to release Nessa but sees the workers and listens. In the single readable rescue action **he holds the manual brake, she shifts the alignment just enough, and the catch seats**. Edda and Pell get through, injured and impatient, not delivering gratitude speeches. Nessa's knowledge and Ansen's free reach have accomplished something together. The detailed load path is for engineering, not a heist sequence on the page.

**Hesk returns.** The operator's provisional name is **Hesk**, replacing Brun because Brun is too close to the main-novella name Bram. Hesk sees the altered station and Ansen attending to Nessa's restraint. He orders Ansen back. Ansen retreats beyond the arm's **usual** work arc, but Hesk engages a **wide direct-manual sweep** that has been shown earlier. Nessa recognizes the control before Ansen does and tries to warn him. Hesk **deliberately commands a seizure** using a powered industrial gripping arm; the frame is not independently choosing a target. Nessa's shoulder is injured resisting its imposed stroke, but her resistance cannot stop the machine. The powered hand crushes Ansen's throat/jaw, killing him quickly. Hesk is responsible because he intentionally applied dangerous machinery against a person; a theatrical murder speech is unnecessary.

**Ending.** After the brief killing, procedural action and Hesk's departure occur before the last image. No report is superimposed on that image; it can occur earlier in the story. A coat can remain on the floor as an understated visual rhyme **only** if physically plausible, not as a final flashback. **Last panel: Nessa held upright in her frame, Ansen dead on the floor beneath her. Nobody else. No dialogue, caption, flashback or work-cycle restart.**

## Creative boundaries

- Preserve explicit, physically credible body horror; avoid accumulating injuries and sound effects solely for shock.
- Dialogue stays ordinary, sometimes petty or awkward. No theme speeches, deathbed confession, repeated catchphrase at the killing or sudden heroic sainthood.
- Ansen is not foolish about machinery: he retreats beyond the **known normal arc** and is caught by a distinct, established wider manual mode.
- Nessa's tiny voluntary motion may warn or trim alignment; it cannot override a powered frame stroke.
- The [Thulan Violence Doctrine](THULAN_VIOLENCE_DOCTRINE.md) applies to graphic novels, alongside [Graphic Novel Style Contract §17](GRAPHIC_NOVEL_STYLE_CONTRACT.md#17-violence-injury-and-gore). No exemption is needed.
- Existing novella chronology and Varek's story remain authoritative; no revised manuscript is asserted.
- Page count, final title, character identities, lighting, sound and all production art remain unapproved.
- The REI/Image Engine proposal and AGY's open Panel Studio PR #3 are outside this literary update.

## 29 October timeline/physics review — candidate fixes, not new author rulings

[Live-manuscript timeline and station-mechanics review V0.1](LABORA_TIMELINE_AND_MECHANICS_REVIEW_V01.md) finds the **Chapter Eleven mention** of the freed miners is a **spoken confirmation**, not another onstage Ansen appearance. Ansen can leave **after his Chapter Ten help**, provided a station is reachable on a short, still-open wash-passage spur; the novella does not yet verify that route. The original six-hour ram estimate is running down by Chapter Ten.

**Critical mechanics finding:** The existing premise that Nessa's restricted shoulder movement shifts the shutter catch is **unproven**. A stronger proposed alternative is for Ansen to work a **load-holding brake** while Nessa watches the catch and signals when he can seat it. That keeps her knowledge essential while removing a contrived physical linkage. Separate the shutter-hoist steam isolation from the still-powered Welded motive line. Hesk's wide sweep, sightlines, grip travel and Nessa's injury are **not** validated. These are review recommendations, not accepted alterations to RULING 037.
