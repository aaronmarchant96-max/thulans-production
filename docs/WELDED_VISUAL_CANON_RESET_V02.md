# The Welded — Visual Canon Reset V0.2

**29 October 2026 · AUTHOR-DIRECTED DESIGN RESET; NO CHARACTER MODEL APPROVED**

**Governing:** [RULING 039](../RULINGS.md#ruling-039-welded-visual-reset) supersedes the prescriptive visual design instructions of [RULING 038](../RULINGS.md#ruling-038-welded-lineage) and the former [Visual Lineage V0.1](WELDED_VISUAL_LINEAGE_V01.md). The authoritative prose, [RULING 035](../RULINGS.md#ruling-035-welded), [RULING 037](../RULINGS.md#ruling-037-labora), [Varek's locks](../RULINGS.md) and the approved graphic-novel style contract are unchanged.

## Why the previous image direction failed

The generated frames kept becoming **wearable steampunk exosuits / robot soldiers**: centred standing mannequin, shoulder armour, symmetrical metal limbs, giant gloves, ornate mask and protruding mouth hose. Adding cylinders and rivets did not cure the underlying mistake. **Every recent generated Welded character design and the side-by-side Varek comparison are rejected as visual references.** A plausible *design premise* is not acceptance of any rendered object.

**The definitive reading is reversed: draw a piece of compulsory labour machinery, then show the captive person restrained inside its working envelope.** The frame is the thing being operated. The person is not its operator.

## What Chapter Six proves (not up for reinvention)

- **Two breaker rigs advance along the track.** Their **breaker bars rise and fall beside the track** and strike the stone. A frame foot drags through ballast.
- A **riveted mask sits low between shoulder braces**. An **eye turns within a slit**. A voice comes through steam: “Can't stop it”; the broken-wrist miner asks for help.
- A **human hand hangs below a cuff** and its swollen fingers jerk as the machine drives. **Do not put every hand into a neat robot glove**: the manuscript demonstrates exposed, helpless human extremities.
- A back-mounted drive continues cycling when snagged. Varek **shears the bar's drive pin**; the breaker drops. He steadies the frame and **closes its steam supply and opens its vent**.
- Kaelen uses tools to free the man: **cut the jaw leather, chisel mask rivets, support head, ease the faceplate off**, leaving raw skin and the feeding-tube notch at the lip. **The human is not biologically welded to the metal.** Sentencing and coercive fastenings make captivity indefinite; physical dismantling remains possible.

These are **source facts**, not illustrative suggestions. Do not revise the manuscript to accommodate a generated suit.

## Identity: what a Welded actually is

A Welded is an **industrial task rig made into a prison**: an external load-bearing frame, serviceable power-and-drive equipment, job-specific work head, and a human restrained where the machine requires their body to be. The operator is a separate handler, outside normal prisoner reach. It can continue its assigned movement when the person resists or becomes unconscious.

The prisoner's body is **not** the main load path for tonnes of machinery. Rails, posts, pivot blocks and anchors take the work reaction; cuffs and harnesses force the unprotected body to follow and absorb painful restraint reactions. This is how the system harms people while still functioning for years.

**Practical moral difference:** Varek's rescue rig gives him the ability to choose where to place his weight; the Welded takes away choice of movement. The modern regime may have **debased the engineering tradition** of protective frames, but a one-to-one match of Varek's helm, chest plate, yoke, arms and feet is **not a visual requirement**. They must not look like miniature Vareks. Exact copying history is still unwritten.

## Visual grammar — intentionally sparse

1. **Work head first:** A breaker bar with its drive and ground reaction; or a gantry/hauling arm with a driven grip. The machine's function should be recognizable before the person is shown.
2. **Human second, but emotionally first:** a small bowed head, cloth-covered shoulders, constrained body, eye, breath and cuffed hands at the work centre. Avoid heroic posture, anatomical display and fetishized bondage imagery.
3. **Two or three dominant structural members:** frame sides, work-axis linkage, and one supported actuation path. No forest of parallel pistons or duplicate arms.
4. **Real fastenings:** bolted/strapped cuffs couple body to driven members, but hands may hang outside the particular cuff as Chapter Six states. Restraint geometry is task-specific.
5. **Service history, not surface noise:** one replacement rivet row, a patched seal, worn leather at contact points, oil only downstream of fittings. Big quiet iron surfaces and black negative space. No uniform rust confetti, brass jewellery, ornamental spikes or fantasy chest emblem.

### Mask, sight and feeding

The existing prose establishes **narrow sight-slits, riveted removable mask, jaw leather and feeding tube**, not a particular fashionable helmet. **Place the head low inside the shoulder restraint**, where an operator and reader can find a human eye. Keep the faceplate plain; leave actual access for tools to loosen it. Use an **unobtrusive flexible feeding line** entering the mouth at a serviceable side fitting, separate from steam/power tubing and from breathing apertures. Never draw a thick gas-mask hose or pressurised piston protruding from a mouth.

**No invention of an exact mask outline is approved yet.** The hero's current head visual lock is an independent matter and must not be redesigned to make these machines match.

## Two machines, not one interchangeable humanoid costume

### A. The mobile breaker rig — Ansen; written Chapter Six

**Read at distance:** paired working breaker bars striking the ground beside the moving track; a low bound human between the shoulders; a bar drive attached to the **frame**, not the human hand. One foot can lag or drag. The mechanism needs a breakable drive pin, reachable steam shutoff and vent, and a chest frame Varek can catch. The exposed human hand hanging below its cuff is a vital close-up.

**Reject:** two monster claws for arms, military exosuit silhouette, arms elegantly free inside shiny metal gauntlets, a permanently floor-bolted base.

### B. The transfer-station hauling rig — Nessa; LABORA development

**Read at distance:** a narrow human restraint position next to a much larger **fixed industrial handling structure**, with **one** plainly powered hauling/gripping attachment. The working arm has a visible normal range and a handler-selected wider range for the story. A gripper can close on an object independently of Nessa's actual fingers. Her arm/shoulder may be held to the driven member, but the tool is **not a giant glove she is wearing**. She remains upright after the handler leaves; the station frame retains her.

**Frame 9** is a working identification, **not** a sacred chest plaque copied from the Gren-Skildus; its **exact attachment position is not yet fixed**. Place it where a worker or clerk would need to read it. The attachment, power branch and controlled reach are resolved by the existing simple [V0.3 staging](LABORA_STATION_BLOCKING_V03.md), not a new engineering dossier.

**Reject:** a copy of Ansen's mobile breaker with a swapped weapon, giant claw-hand costume, stacked duplicate pumps, exposed human made to pose beside an otherwise independent robot.

## Varek relationship — preserve the theme, stop the literal costume copying

Varek is **eight feet tall**, a deeply integrated rescue/anchor machine with a human-protective hull and established dimensions/silhouette authority. A Welded is **cheap, modern, serviceable and physically dismantlable**, built to impose work on a prisoner. Their moral inversion is essential. Their exact visible component-for-component genealogy is **not**.

The most compelling rhyme is **behavior**: Varek steadies a failing load so someone can live. A Welded advances and keeps striking after its occupant is hurt. If a comparative illustration looks like "gold heroic robot versus silver evil robot," it fails.

## Art and approval rules

- Draw a **single labour-action silhouette** for each machine before any three-view sheet, close-up or cover.
- Show its **tool, anchor/track, constrained human position and one visible drive link** with simple unshaded shapes. It should read without labels.
- Then one 3/4 illustration with **dark comic ink**: structural blacks, selective hatch on materials, minimal detail on the person's face and hands, disciplined wear, readable warm/cold separation.
- Only after that, test the mask's feeding access, Varek's rescue sequence, and Hesk's externally imposed grip in sequential panels. No engineering calculations beyond what a reader needs to believe.
- **No generated image, including any image already shown in chat, is approved canon or a design lock.** A new approval requires an explicit author decision based on a visual sheet.

## Rejection gate — six questions

1. Is this **a working machine with a captive**, or somebody wearing a costume?
2. Can the imposed job continue while the human resists?
3. Can we locate **the work head, frame load path, restrained person, and handler-controlled drive**?
4. Can Varek plausibly stop and steady the breaker while Kaelen frees Ansen as written?
5. Can Nessa's powered gripper move independent of her consent while her body stays constrained?
6. Does it look like a particular brutal **industrial institution**, not generic steampunk, Warhammer, robot armour or an iron maiden?

**No next-generation prompt until a first unrendered silhouette passes the first four questions.** If it fails, fix the underlying design premise—not the texture.

## Boundaries
