# Thulan Violence Doctrine

**Updated:** 27 October 2026. Applies to prose, graphic-novel panels, and animation.
The accepted manuscript governs written events. The [novella blueprint](NOVELLA_BLUEPRINT.md),
[RULING 035](../RULINGS.md#ruling-035-welded), and the [Salvation direction](SALVATION_STORY_DIRECTION.md)
record their respective story scopes. This document governs treatment; it does not
establish new deaths or revise the manuscript.

## 1. The Governing Principle

> **"The violence is fast. Understanding what happened is slow."**

In the Deephearth, human bodies exist in the same physical space as heavy, uncompromising industrial machinery and pneumatic weaponry. Violence is never romantic, choreographic, or drawn-out for spectacle. It does not linger on sadism; it demonstrates consequence.

---

### Before impact: a life worth returning to

Give people ordinary life that is pleasurable to witness: shared food, useful work,
private humour, minor disagreements, familiar gestures, and plans. Tenderness needs
room to exist beyond explaining a later death. A character needs preferences, skills,
and relationships beyond the injury the plot will inflict.

Let gains be real. A rescue can succeed; a repair can work; a reunion can restore
something the characters thought lost. The audience should have reasons to imagine a
future with them. Constant suffering makes the next injury predictable and can reduce
the people to a succession of wounds.

Emotional debt is the audience's attachment to a particular shared life, not an
obligation to kill a character after a kind gesture. When loss comes, the reader can
miss a person's company and the future that had become imaginable.

## 2. The Three Beats of Violence

Every violent event in Thulan narrative and animation follows three distinct stages:

```
[1. Impact]       -->   [2. Recognition]   -->   [3. Residue]
Instantaneous            Mechanical               Permanent physical
kinetic trauma           comprehension            evidence & constraint
```

### Beat 1: Impact
* The injury occurs suddenly, often between eye-blinks or within a single mechanical stroke.
* Establish the machine's movement and the character's vulnerability clearly enough
  that the cause is readable. Do not announce every impending injury through repeated
  threat inserts, ominous captions, or dramatic slow motion. Suspense and a character's
  warning are permitted; prolonging the fatal stroke for spectacle is not.
* Show where the force goes: through a restraint, joint, tool, structure, or body.
  Weight, support, and remaining pressure matter. Armour does not make force vanish.

### Beat 2: Recognition
* The audience (and surviving characters) understands exactly what the machinery or weapon did.
* The physical mechanics are plausible, legible, and unyielding.
* The machinery often continues its cycle regardless of the person killed or severed.
* Understanding includes the people: who is trying to help, who has control, and when
  someone realizes the intended rescue has failed. Recognition may arrive after the
  injury; do not stretch the injury itself to give everyone an explanatory speech.
* In a Welded encounter, distinguish imposed motion from the occupant's intention.
  An involuntary attack does not make the prisoner a willing killer. External commands
  do not erase the responsibility of the person issuing them.

### Beat 3: Residue
* Physical evidence remains in the environment and alters subsequent choices.
* Bodies do not despawn. Blood and soot stain stone. Broken gear jams tolerances.
* Survivors must physically navigate the aftermath to continue.
* Relationships and unfinished tasks remain: a hand still trying to reach someone,
  a prepared place never occupied, work that another person must take over. Give the
  survivor an action or need beyond simply displaying grief.
* Other lives can continue nearby without making this loss insignificant. An encounter
  that occupies a few lines in Varek's story can contain another person's whole future.
  Administrative brevity is a limit of the record, not a measure of human worth.

## 3. Canonical Voice & Tonal Touchstones

* *"The shield stopped the hammer. His ribs did not."*
* *"The lift took his arm at the elbow. It reached the next tier on schedule."*
* *"The piston fired before his hand cleared the collar. His gauntlet remained closed. Everything inside it became flat."*
* *"The machinery never noticed the flesh. It simply finished the stroke."*

---

## 4. Strict Constraints & Prohibitions

1. **No Torture Spectacle**: Violence is industrial, operational, or desperate—never gratuitous torture porn.
2. **No Endless Screaming for Spectacle**: Vocal response follows the specific injury
   and circumstances. A cry, lost breath, involuntary sound, or silence may be
   appropriate. Do not claim that every severe injury produces the same response or
   use prolonged screaming as a substitute for understanding the person.
3. **No Action-Movie Quips**: Characters do not deliver one-liners before or after taking a life. Breathing and checking seals take precedence.
4. **No Physically Impossible Survival**: Armor protects against fragmentation and glancing blows; it does not nullify hydraulic crushing or direct kinetic shockwaves.

---

### Sound and panel rhythm

Gentle music may continue through a violent action. A familiar sung phrase or gesture
can preserve the relationship while machinery destroys the possibility of continuing
it. A horror sting, triumphant swell, or cut to silence is a choice, not a mandatory
signal. Original lyrics and gestures should be developed together; no reference song
or exact lyric timing is prescribed here.

In a graphic novel, page turns, matched compositions, panel size, and restrained
lettering can perform the same work. Allow warmth and recognition time on the page;
the fatal movement can occupy a much shorter interval. Repetition should reveal a
change, as in a successful restraint release mirrored by a failed loaded-pin attempt.

### Salvation application — pilot scope

Ansen and Nessa share ordinary warmth before their confinement. Their reunion and
initial progress at a fastening give them a real reason to hope. When he cannot free
the loaded coupling, Nessa tries to send him away. The commanded grip kills him while
she resists. Keep the action brief, its control legible, and her subsequent attempts
to reach him human. Do not diagnose his trauma merely to explain away his choices.

The equipment finding **FRAME 9. NO FAULT FOUND.** concerns whether the mechanism
obeyed its controls. The [16-page pilot](SALVATION_PILOT_16_PAGES.md) answers its opening
with the completed inspection entry before returning to the coat prepared for her.
Frame 9, its handler, and the gallery are explicitly labelled pilot staging; this
example does not insert those scenes into the existing novella or settle final
mechanical geometry.

## 5. Thulan Warrior Identity & The Metric of Honor

The martial ethic of the Thulans is rooted in Varek’s maxim:

> **"Strength is measured by what you stand between."**

* **The Measure of Worth**: Thulan warriors take pride in holding elevator throats, bridges, pressure doors, aquifers, ventilation trunks, and civilian retreat routes.
* **Accounting**: Honor is not tallied in enemy body counts, but in **minutes held** and **civilians passed to safety**.
* **Council Corruption**: The tragic arc of the Thulan warrior caste is the Council's steady weaponization of this courage:
  $$\text{“Hold for your Hearth”} \longrightarrow \text{“Die where Provision assigns you.”}$$

This protective ethic also appears in ordinary care, maintenance, and the work of
people who cannot physically hold a passage. A person's worth does not depend on
strength, military usefulness, or whether their attempt to help succeeds.
