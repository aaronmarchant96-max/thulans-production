# The Thulans — Rebellion Novellas: Series Direction

**Updated:** 24 October 2026  
**Authority:** Aaron Marchant's approved continuation direction; [RULING 034](../RULINGS.md#ruling-034-rebellion).  
**Status:** The ascending series premise is approved. The working book structure below is provisional. No sequel prose, final book count, titles, numbered floor map, rebellion start date, or ultimate political outcome is locked.

## The series promise

The rebellion rises through the Mountain. Each novella follows a working community's response to the pressure coming from below, until the struggle reaches the Spire for the final climax.

Each community changes the rebellion as it receives it. People have different protections, loyalties, knowledge, and reasons to fear disruption. A new level needs its own protagonist, immediate material problem, consequential choice, and local resolution. Familiar people, documents, repairs, and institutions carry consequences between books.

The Mountain's geography provides the structure. The story remains about people deciding what they will bear for one another and what they will refuse to impose on others.

## Cultural injury and political consequence

The rulers take an old Thulan's defiance and teach children that it was obedience. The injury is cultural as well as material: fellowship, protective duty, skilled work, names, and sacrifice are repurposed to demand compliance.

Varek is a living connection to an older Thulan ethic during the first novella. Do not promote him into the last surviving Thulan or the only remaining bearer of culture without a separate ruling. The culture remains alive in the Hearths: people share work, preserve names, maintain tools, wipe the rim, and care for those who cannot carry their own weight.

Stolen water, debt, expendable labor, and exclusion from the arks provide material grievances. The false monument gives those grievances a shared person and public symbol. It does not automatically make every worker a rebel.

Truth moves through copies, shifts, workshops, and domestic relationships. Publication alone does not instantly produce agreement or revolution. Bram's fidelity matters on every side: a rebel account can also become false, and preserving Varek's meaning can require challenging allies.

## Working ascent — development scaffold, not a locked volume list

These are social and functional bands. They are not numbered tiers or approved titles.

| Working band | Local dramatic problem | How the rebellion could change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lower workings and Hearths | Families need water, drainage, shelter, and reliable care. The first novella provides the originating wound and surviving evidence. | Survival and private witness begin to create relationships that can support collective action. |
| Haulage, incline, and foundry communities | Crews sustain the transport and production systems on which the whole Mountain depends. Guild protection and access to escape may divide them. | Grievance becomes organized labor leverage, bargaining, and disagreement about what must keep running. |
| Registries and administrative communities | Clerks and junior officials authorize movements, allocations, identities, and records. They have households and exposure of their own. | Concealment, correction, complicity, and selective refusal affect what the state can enforce or make people believe. |
| Spire and highest authority | Those directing the systems face the people whose cooperation sustains them. | The final climax concerns authority and a viable way to maintain life under real scarcity. Seizing an office alone cannot settle it. |

A band may take more than one novella; multiple physical floors may share a book. Exact order, floor numbering, character roster, titles, volume count, and plot outcomes need separate author decisions.

## Shared life-support constraint

The Mountain is a sealed, materially finite environment. Water, breathable air, heat, drainage, transport, food, and maintenance link every class to the work of others.

The workers' leverage comes with obligations. A disruption can hurt a nursery, a medical delivery, or another shift before it hurts the rulers. The narrative should distinguish withholding particular production or official cooperation from indiscriminate destruction of shared life support. Give each group an argument about the people who will bear its choice.

Examples such as escalating boiler temperatures, destroying ventilation, or starving the summit are brainstorming possibilities, not approved acts, operating methods, or inevitable plot beats. Do not turn technical spectacle into the series' definition of resistance.

Some upper-level people will depend on coercion; some will be frightened workers; some lower-level people will defend the existing arrangements. Avoid a vertical ladder of uniformly worse villains. The question of who governs must remain connected to who can keep people alive.

## Character inheritance

- **Bram:** evidence, accurate copying, and the move from private witness toward collective reading. A later political role is open, not a locked “leader of the rebellion” appointment.
- **Sanna:** practical organization and the culture of keeping people alive. She remains a person with family obligations, not merely the rebellion's moral symbol.
- **Kaelen and the working crews:** workmanship, apprenticeships, repairs, shared material history, and competing loyalties. Exact sequel roles remain open.
- **Haila, Tomas, and Malakor:** retain their established relationships and individual acts. No future office, faction, survival, or allegiance is assigned by this document.
- **Varek:** remains dead. His work and memory can be interpreted, appropriated, corrected, and fought over. Resurrection does not supply the climax.

The separate proposed Varek recovery-arc edit plan concerns the first novella's pacing of understanding through dialogue. It has not been applied by this series-direction update and does not authorize changing established facts or erasing his early humanity.

## Chronology and geography decisions still open

The [chronology working model](REBELLION_CHRONOLOGY_WORKING_MODEL.md) records the proposed bridge: a costly managed retreat during the six-year interval, a first ark approaching readiness at the dedication, and departure preparations creating the sequel's immediate pressure. It also proposes Theodemir's continued tenure and a maintenance-crew transfer as a possible conflict. These are development proposals; precise dates, geography, engineering assumptions, and sequel events remain unapproved.

1. The monument and Bram's new invitation occur six years after Varek's death. Decide whether the next novella begins there, develops resistance during the intervening years, or uses another explicitly dated structure.
2. Do not move the monument, death, or existing family chronology to make an outline easier.
3. Establish how the magma threat, ark construction, reserve allocations, and remaining habitable infrastructure relate to that interval before introducing a precise new deadline. No fixed launch date or six-year eruption countdown has been approved.
4. Reconcile the older numbered strata table with the written office on Tier Nine. “Lower Four,” Drift 14, Shaft 44, hearth numbers, and tier numbers are different labels; do not equate them by arithmetic.
5. Exact social-band boundaries, travel routes, state and merchant jurisdictions, and where daylight is accessible require a coherent map. Do not automatically portray every administrative worker as living in sunlight.

## Scope of older collapse plans

Earlier lore charts have Varek exposing the lie publicly, surviving the ensuing civil collapse, and regretting the consequences before his final descent. That is not the chronology of the accepted twelve-chapter novella.

For this literary continuation, [RULING 034](../RULINGS.md#ruling-034-rebellion) separates the current book from that earlier macro outline. The new series does not inherit its exact weeks-long collapse timetable, a predetermined catastrophic finale, Varek's continued survival, or the decades-later residual-machine sequence.

Real scarcity, consent, human responsibility, and the possibility of costly failure remain governing concerns. This clarification does not erase separately scoped cinematic, music, engineering, or game material.
