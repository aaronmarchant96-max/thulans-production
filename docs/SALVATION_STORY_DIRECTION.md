## Relationship and first act

**Ansen and Nessa are unrelated orphans**, raised in the same lower service passages. At roughly **13**, they share hot stolen bread and an ill-fitting coat, compete, irritate one another and imagine ordinary comforts. Their early life needs enough uninterrupted space to be enjoyable on its own; don't introduce every happy memory solely to set up a fatal callback. **No childhood romance.**

They are arrested for stealing bread from the upper Spire and separately sentenced to **three months** in powered penal frames. Five years later neither has been formally released. At about **18**, their reunion may carry an uncertain new attraction, without speeches, a confession, or premature certainty. Ages, chronology and precise line readings remain provisional where the authoritative novella does not settle them.

Ansen isn't uniformly the strong rescuer. Nessa isn't a passive waiting victim. He can be injured, proud, annoyed, stubborn and funny; she can be angry, resentful, observant and occasionally petty. They must discover each other again rather than immediately perform a tragic love story.

**Reunion dialogue:** An awkward hair observation—"Your hair." / "What about it?" / "It's terrible."—is a candidate, not locked balloons. Avoid stacking all the bread, coat, cold-feet and "half each" memories in one scene. Any real intimacy should emerge while they work together.

## Nessa's five-year working life

Nessa serves in a **conscious, externally powered hauling frame**. Its mechanical motion overrides resistance; feeding hardware, restricted speech, bruises, chafing and shoulder load make confinement physically legible. She can brace, hum or perform small voluntary shifts **within restraint play**, but cannot stop imposed industrial movement.

In the working treatment **Pell** is the younger worker she warns by short hums; **Edda** is an older rigger whose care of feed lines/padding is practical, sometimes irritated. Edda initially knows her only as "Nine". These supporting roles are provisional companion characters, not new facts inserted into the novella. The opening worker Nessa helps should be one of the later endangered workers, establishing why the rescue matters without a thematic speech. Other people have their own priorities and are not props invented to advertise Nessa's virtue.

## Source continuity, lead and pursuit

**Ansen is the first broken-wrist miner freed by Varek and Kaelen in Chapter Six.** Nessa is not the second freed miner. His help in **Chapter Ten** and confirmation of both miners' freedom in **Chapter Eleven** must be respected. He cannot disappear from those events for an implausible long search. The novella's approaching rams create a **short evacuation window**.

The favored clue is **one spoken lead**, relying on the remembered intake assignment ("boy to breakers, girl to transfer hauls") and an overheard report that transfer hauls are still running. Don't invent his literacy, a records office or hours of false leads. Exact gallery route, timing and availability of the witness must be checked.

## Joint success, not a mechanical heist

During station shutdown **Edda is injured beyond a drop shutter** and Pell stays with her. Ansen finds Nessa and first wants to free her. She directs him toward them. He listens; she knows the machinery and he has free reach.

On the page show **one comprehensible mechanism**: Ansen holds the brake; Nessa shifts the alignment through limited voluntary play; the catch engages so the shutter can remain open. Edda and Pell evacuate, in pain and in a hurry. It should feel like work they manage together, not a puzzle exposition or celebratory sacrifice.

**Do not assert a verified load path.** Station V0.2 must be checked so the brake bears shutter weight independently; Nessa is not secretly supporting the gate with muscle or a locked shoulder.

## Operator, coercion and violent end

The handler's selected **working treatment name is Hesk**, replacing **Brun** because Brun resembled canonical **Bram**. The proposed station operator sees unauthorized interference and orders Ansen back. Ansen steps beyond the arm's **usual work arc**, reasonably believing it clear. Hesk deliberately switches into an **established wider manual sweep**, commands the frame to seize him, and the gripper reaches him. Nessa recognizes the mode and warns/resists, but has no control over the driven stroke. Hesk is accountable for choosing potentially lethal machinery, not a theatrical villain or an autonomous machine.

Ansen dies **quickly** from the forced grip. Nessa's shoulder bears a visible cost from opposing it. Graphic physical consequences are acceptable when motivated, but do not pile on multiple cinematic injuries merely to manufacture a response. The doctrine's principle is **fast violence; slow understanding**.

After Hesk has concluded whatever immediate procedure is necessary, the **final image is only Nessa trapped upright and Ansen dead on the floor beneath her.** No handler, title card, overlay, last speech, flashback, work-cycle restart or promise of rescue. A believable coat in the environment may quietly echo childhood, but don't force it.
