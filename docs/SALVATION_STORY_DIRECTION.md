# The Thulans: Salvation — Ansi and Nasa

**Updated:** 27 October 2026
**Status:** Author-approved characters, penal-system premise, and tragic outcome. Pilot staging, shot order, exact ages, runtime, and mechanical design remain in development.
**Authority:** [RULING 035](../RULINGS.md#ruling-035-welded); companion to the [rebellion direction](REBELLION_SAGA_DIRECTION.md).

## People and sentence

Ansi (AHN-see) and his sister Nasa (NAH-sah) were caught stealing bread from the upper Spire. Both received three-month Welded sentences. Five years later they remain confined, without an announced extension or effective avenue of appeal. Exact birthdays and Nasa's seniority are not locked. Ansi must remain consistent with the manuscript's young miner, no older than nineteen-year-old Wulfila.

The Spire subjects people it deems unfit for society to service inside powered frames instead of imprisonment. A term and release date provide the appearance of a temporary sentence. Ordinary completion does not secure release: nobody returns through the promised process. Physical rescue, such as Varek freeing Ansi, is an exception to that confinement, not evidence that the release system works. The specific administrative means of concealing expired terms remains open.

The notch inside Ansi's lower lip is a pressure injury from the feeding apparatus. His mouth and jaw are constrained; speech is painful and limited, not wholly impossible. Preserve his existing pleas. He cannot reach officials or obtain a hearing merely because he can force out a few words.

Name provenance is recorded in the [language note](THULAN_LANGUAGE_NOTE.md). Neither name is presented as an attested historical Gothic personal name.

## Existing rescue and continuity boundary

Ansi is the first Welded miner in Chapter Six, with the broken wrist. Varek shears the breaker drive pin, catches the frame, closes the steam valve, and says: “It's stopped. I have you.” Kaelen frees the mask. Both miners survive that encounter; the person Varek kills is the guard aiming at Bram.

The author-supplied current manuscript export also shows the freed young miner offering his good hand to help in Chapter Ten. Chapter Eleven confirms that both men are out of their frames. Preserve those appearances. Ansi's departure to search for Nasa must be placed after that established presence; the early pilot's immediate departure after extraction is not a settled chronology. Nasa is not a replacement identity for the second miner Varek also frees.

The export was supplied in the development conversation; this update does not claim a new independent read of the live Google Doc or synchronize repository prose mirrors. The time and route of Ansi's later search, and its relation to the evacuation and Varek's death, need an explicit continuity pass before the pilot is locked.

## Approved tragedy

Ansi finds Nasa still trapped in another frame. Varek's rescue has made escape imaginable. Ansi attempts to do for her what Varek did for him, while recent trauma, urgency, and their relationship compromise his judgement of his own strength and the machinery. This is a dramatic account of his behaviour, not a clinical diagnosis.

He makes an effort to release the coupling pins but cannot free them. A loaded connection is not a loose quick-release pin: Varek sheared a drive pin with a thirty-kilogram maul, whereas Ansi has a broken wrist and limited tools. Those pins and loads must be designed visibly before production rather than assumed mechanically proven.

When Ansi realizes he cannot release her, he looks into her face. They make eye contact; tears run down her cheek. The powered grip forces her hand around his throat and kills him. Nasa attempts to resist and warn him; she does not choose the killing. A handler directing the seizure is the current control premise. Lever positions, travel, grip geometry, and injury timing remain design work.

Nasa remains conscious and confined through the immediate aftermath. Her eventual fate is open. Ansi's death is not the cause of the whole rebellion, and Varek need not learn of it. A brief encounter in Varek's story can be a life-changing event in someone else's.

## Emotional and visual direction

- Give the siblings ordinary shared life before separating them: mutual care, teasing, food, warmth, habits, and plans. Nasa needs her own personality and actions. Neither is merely a device to motivate the other.
- Allow their reunion to recover something real. Recognition and a familiar gentle touch need time to be enjoyed before the failed rescue. The audience should begin imagining their future together.
- Violence happens quickly. Understanding and the surviving sister's attempts to respond take longer. Follow the [violence doctrine](THULAN_VIOLENCE_DOCTRINE.md); avoid a prolonged display of torture.
- Scale matters: these people have complete lives even when an official account or another protagonist's story gives them one line. Other urgent work continues outside their gallery.
- The proposed opening record is **FRAME 9. NO FAULT FOUND.** It describes equipment obeying its controls; it does not establish that nobody was harmed. Its placement over black, without revealing the death, is the latest pilot proposal.
- A coat prepared as a place to lower Nasa's head is the proposed closing image. After five years in custody, do not assume Ansi still owns his childhood coat; a borrowed worker's coat can repeat the earlier act of sharing warmth.

## Form, music, and reference scope

The initial format is a graphic-novel/illustrated short developed through still images, modest motion, and layered 2.5D shots. A film version can guide panels without making runtime a fixed requirement for the graphic novel.

Two structures have been explored: reverse fragments intercut with Varek's forward rescue, and a later mostly forward pilot that gives the siblings more time together and keeps the fatal sequence uninterrupted. The latter is the current working draft, not a locked final edit. If reverse passages return, each needs a visible temporal cue such as rising drops, returning steam, or a tool lifting into a hand.

The author's central reference is the 2006 Gears of War “Mad World” trailer: a gentle handling of a broken classical statue face carries the loss of a civilization, while Gary Jules's restrained vocal gives the action weariness and human vulnerability. Specific lyric/gesture relationships matter alongside overall mood. No exact word-to-frame cue sheet has been verified in this session, and no music has been selected for release. Develop an original work-song/score around meaningful words, pauses, breath, and gesture; test keeping its tenderness through the fatal action rather than automatically switching to a horror sting or silence.

Dead Island's 2011 reveal informs retrospective recognition and the hope of a rescue whose outcome is known. Love, Death & Robots informs compact character attachment and abrupt loss; Invincible and The Boys inform readable physical consequences and delayed recognition. These are references for analysis, not approved shot copies or changes to the Thulan violence doctrine.
