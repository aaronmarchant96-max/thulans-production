## Aim

Make the reader enjoy Ansen and Nessa's company, actively follow his search, believe in their reunion, and understand the loss of a future they had begun to imagine. The relationship should sustain interest before music or violence is added. The eventual score remains part of the intended emotional experience.

The present draft has a strong outcome but moves too neatly between isolated emotional images. Revision 4 should carry a continuous pursuit through an inhabited place. It should give Nessa more individuality and let sibling humour turn naturally into private care under pressure.

## 1. Establish the people before arranging the tragedy

Write a short private character sheet for each sibling: a useful skill, a source of pride, a habit that irritates the other, and something they enjoy together. Give both a chance to be capable, wrong, funny, and protective. Nessa is not always the parent and Ansen is not always the dependent child.

Choose a few shared experiences that serve the opening's immediate action. The theft and search for a place to eat can carry their competition, familiarity with workers and routes, and practical care. Every affectionate act need not be a warning of death.

Test "Shift over" or "Let me see" as a possible recurring phrase. Select an anchor only if it sounds natural in different exchanges; do not adopt either automatically or force a final repetition during the killing.

**Output:** A compact relationship sheet and opening scene choices, labelled as draft choices rather than new canon until incorporated.

## 2. Draft the experience in continuous prose

Write the opening, rescue/search, and reunion/escape in prose before dividing them into panels. Keep the viewpoint close to what Ansen notices and tries to do. Use familiar work, shift rhythms, neighbours, food, and routes when they affect his attention or actions.

Bring Nessa into his purpose as soon as he can communicate after rescue. His listening, questions, and attempts to locate her should continue through Lower Four while he still helps the other freed miner. Do not invent a convenient false report or erase his Chapter Ten/Eleven presence to hurry him away.

Let discoveries change decisions: he can speak, but has no location; he finds her, but cannot yet free her; they release a restraint, but the load-bearing pin resists. Allow genuine gains and moments of pleasure within that movement.

Use the three supplied John Saul pages for close comparison of pace and viewpoint, not a formula for copying sentences. His late family revelation is known here through the author's recollection; no equivalent secret is prescribed for this story. Further Koontz reading is useful when an excerpt is available, but is not a prerequisite to drafting.

**Output:** A readable prose treatment without camera instructions explaining what every gesture should make the audience feel.

## 4. Check the scene and the reading experience

Make rough silhouettes or diagrams for the gallery before final art. Establish the handler, command path, frame travel, loaded connection, Nessa's small voluntary gestures, and the valve Ansen cannot reach. Reconcile their route with the established later novella appearances. This is blocking work; no frozen physics code needs changing and no engineering validation should be claimed.

Read the script aloud and review it without a score. On a reader pass, identify:

- Where the reader wants the next encounter to happen, and where that desire stalls.
- What they remember about each sibling beyond the injuries.
- When Nessa's aim changes from escaping to getting Ansen away.
- Whether they understand who controls the fatal movement.
- What future they expected the siblings might recover.

Use those responses to revise. Do not treat a required tear, a fixed number of laughs, or a questionnaire score as proof that the story works.

**Output:** A revised script and thumbnails that can be followed without an explanatory commentary.

## 5. Develop sound alongside a rough animatic

Compose an original work-song and record a scratch read at natural speed. Develop meaningful words, pauses, and gestures together. Choose actual pitches and cue positions from that work rather than claiming the three-note motif is already composed.

Test the gentle phrase continuing through the command and death. Leave room for cloth, tools, breathing, the valve, and ordinary work beyond the gallery. Do not inherit Eleven Bells music, bell cues, timing, or playback changes.

Use simple boards before commissioning or generating final images. Measure the resulting edit, revise the balance between movement and held attention, and then enter real timing. No runtime is locked in this plan.

**Output:** A review animatic and measured timing when the required scratch assets exist. These are later deliverables, not files supplied by this planning update.
