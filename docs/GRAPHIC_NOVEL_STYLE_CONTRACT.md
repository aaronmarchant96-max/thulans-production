# Thulans Graphic-Novel Style Contract

## 1. Purpose

This contract turns the approved Thulan visual direction into production rules.
It exists to prevent three failures:

1. technically competent panels that look like generic grimdark;
2. beautiful panels that bury the human cost beneath machinery;
3. images that imply canon, physics, or approval the evidence does not support.

The governing image is not a warrior standing above a civilization.

It is a tired person trying to finish necessary work while the civilization
presses down around them.

## 2. Status language

- **LOCKED:** directly controlled by an existing ruling or canonical source.
- **APPROVED DIRECTION:** previously accepted visual or narrative direction.
- **PROVISIONAL:** a production setting requiring calibration against the proof
  page before it becomes a fixed render profile.
- **UNIMPLEMENTED:** the rule is written but no executable tool currently
  enforces it.

Detail does not convert a provisional decision into canon.

## 3. Visual thesis

### The short form

> Administrative dread. Industrial tenderness. Consequences that remain.

### The full form

The Thulans inhabit an ancient industrial civilization that has converted
endurance, rescue, faith, accounting, and sacrifice into machinery for managing
decline. The art must show that corruption through ordinary rooms, exhausted
bodies, repaired objects, rationed light, and infrastructure built for more
people than remain.

The Mountain is vast, but it is never the emotional protagonist.

RULING 001 remains supreme:

> Never let the Mountain become bigger than the people crushed beneath it.

### Five visual pillars

1. **Human before monument.** A hand, face, injury, hesitation, or domestic
   habit gives scale to the system.
2. **Weight has a path.** Machines transfer load through visible structures,
   bodies compress, feet plant, timbers bend, and surfaces remember contact.
3. **Institutions leave objects.** Debt, quota, and piety appear as bands,
   seals, ledgers, tools, ration hooks, clothing, wounds, and altered rooms.
4. **Everything has been maintained too long.** Repairs accumulate without
   returning anything to new condition.
5. **Darkness has jurisdiction.** Black space is structural, political, and
   material. It is not a filter applied to every image.

## 4. Influence boundary

Use functional lessons, never surface imitation.

| Reference | Transferable mechanism | Do not copy |
|---|---|---|
| *Watchmen* | stable institutional rhythm; rupture has meaning | exact nine-panel page grammar, costumes, color scripting |
| *Maus* | ordinary objects carry history and guilt | character abstraction or visual shorthand |
| *Persepolis* | immediate human clarity under political pressure | graphic surface treatment or character design |
| *Hellboy* | black mass, silhouette, negative space, architectural weight | Mike Mignola's line, shapes, faces, or page compositions |
| *The Arrival* | silent systems explained through environment and behavior | Shaun Tan's creature, architecture, or texture language |
| *Akira* | spatial clarity before destruction and motion | vehicles, city language, manga rendering, or panel copies |
| *Saga* | intimate relationships anchor enormous systems | character styling, color treatment, or space-opera imagery |
| *Sin City* | disciplined value control and rare accent color | Frank Miller's silhouettes, rain language, or page designs |

No prompt, brief, or handoff may request a page “in the style of” a named
artist. Describe the required visual function.

## 5. Identity hierarchy

Every panel is read in this order:

1. human face, hand, posture, breath, or bodily consequence;
2. immediate physical decision;
3. Varek or dominant machine silhouette;
4. institutional evidence;
5. architecture and work process;
6. texture.

If the eye lands on background rivets before it understands the decision, the
panel fails.

If Varek's size erases the person beside him, the staging fails.

If the panel could belong to any dieselpunk or science-fiction setting after
removing one logo, the Thulan identity fails.

## 6. Page grammar

### Baseline grid

Use a six-unit page grid. The normal page resolves into three columns with
variable row heights. This supports ledgers, work routines, inspections,
rituals, queues, lifts, and other institutional repetition without copying a
fixed nine-panel structure.

Routine remains aligned. Pressure enters through small violations:

- a figure touches a gutter;
- smoke crosses a border;
- a ledger edge continues between panels;
- Varek's shoulder or yoke cannot fit the assigned frame.

### Grid rupture

Break the grid only when:

- architecture physically fails;
- Varek cannot fit the institution's frame;
- concealed evidence becomes undeniable;
- violence destroys routine;
- a character loses agency;
- the true scale of the Mountain must overwhelm a human viewpoint.

The first rupture on a page must be readable as an event. A page that begins
chaotic and remains chaotic has no visual pressure curve.

### Page rhythm

Each page needs:

- one orientation panel;
- one human decision;
- one physical consequence or evidence token;
- one controlled pause;
- one reason to cross the page turn.

Not every item requires its own panel. A strong panel can perform several jobs.

### Silent read

Every page must communicate its core physical action with balloons and captions
hidden. Dialogue may deepen motive or reveal contradiction. It may not repair
unclear staging.

## 7. Composition and camera

### Human-scale composition

- Establish geography from a worker's reachable space, not an impossible aerial
  overview.
- Let ceilings, door frames, desks, lift cages, and support posts measure bodies.
- Show exits, hazards, supports, and witnesses before a major action.
- Reserve extreme scale views for moments when scale changes a decision.
- Protect negative space for lettering before rendering detail.

### Lens families — provisional until proof-page calibration

- **35–50 mm equivalent:** default human dialogue, labor, and institutional
  observation.
- **24–28 mm equivalent:** low, close spatial pressure and vertical anxiety;
  use sparingly and keep Varek's proportions credible.
- **70–85 mm equivalent:** evidence inserts, hands, bands, seals, ledger marks,
  injuries, and constrained facial reaction.

Do not use wide lenses merely to make Varek look heroic. Low angle means burden,
obstruction, or threatened structure before it means dominance.

### Depth

Build panels in three readable layers:

1. foreground obstruction or evidence;
2. human decision plane;
3. institutional or architectural pressure.

Smoke and dust may separate layers. They may not hide missing geometry,
incorrect contact, or unreadable staging.

## 8. Value and lighting

### Three-band requirement

Before texture, every panel resolves into:

- structural dark;
- material midtone;
- focal light.

The focal light belongs to a face, hand, action, or evidence token. It does not
belong automatically to the largest machine.

### Squint test

At thumbnail size, a panel must retain:

- one dominant dark mass;
- one focal light shape;
- subject/background separation;
- readable head and hands when emotionally necessary;
- a clear path through the panel.

If several lamps compete, remove or suppress all but the motivated source.

### Motivated light

Warm sources are physical and local:

- kerosene lantern;
- forge mouth;
- furnace inspection port;
- hot metal;
- emergency flame;
- active tool heat.

Cold illumination comes from shaft daylight, mineral bounce, inspection lamps,
wet stone, or distant electrical work light. There is no ambient amber wash.

### Darkness

Use solid charcoal for inaccessible shafts, dead chambers, sealed records, and
institutional void. Preserve some untextured black. Filling every dark area with
detail weakens the Mountain.

## 9. Palette

### Core colors

| Function | Color |
|---|---|
| Deep bedrock | `#12171A` |
| Charcoal structure | `#1C2326` |
| Soot linen | `#A6A092` |
| Bone and parchment | `#D8D2C0` |
| Lantern and forge | `#E8A13C` |
| Hot flame | `#FFB040` |
| Oxide and dried blood | `#B04A3C` |
| Fresh ember and acute heat | `#FF4500` |
| Lead bands | `#687178` |
| Audit and cold work light | `#96A8B9` |
| Weathered brass | `#DA9B30` |
| Old copper | `#915C18` |

### Color law

- Amber means heat, shelter, labor, or evidence demanding attention.
- Lead blue means administrative control, audit, cold ventilation, or distance.
- Ember red means acute heat, injury, rupture, or irreversible danger.
- Gren-Skildus green is a familial relic color. It is not a faction-wide neon
  accent and must remain mineral, worn, and irregular.
- Blue-white discharge belongs only to an active Faírg-Tygil event and must be
  accompanied by load consequence: shudder, venting, deflection, ground bite,
  or bodily strain.
- Violet is a failure state for dying arcs and melting copper, not a general
  mystical color.

Large saturated areas require a story reason. Color must not make the world
look cleaner or more technologically advanced than it is.

## 10. Materials and surface history

### Primary material family

- hammered cast iron;
- blackened rolled steel;
- oxidized bronze and brass;
- limestone, basalt, and calcite dust;
- wet hydraulic residue;
- coal soot and furnace scale;
- heavy timber shoring;
- split hide, rolled linen, wool padding, and patched work cloth;
- parchment, slate, wax, lead, and iron accounting hardware.

### Wear logic

Wear follows contact and service:

- polished edges where hands and straps repeatedly pass;
- soot accumulation downstream of vents;
- oil below seals and hose crimps;
- bright scrape at recent metal contact;
- mineral bloom at damp joints;
- crushed fibers at load-bearing cloth;
- repair plates that differ in age, alloy, rivet spacing, and workmanship.

Do not scatter identical scratches across a surface. Random wear is noise, not
history.

### Forbidden surface language

- pristine military plating;
- decorative science-fiction panel lines;
- chrome or glossy black armor;
- emissive seams without a physical source;
- glowing runes, crystals, or magic residue;
- identical factory weathering across unrelated objects;
- clean steampunk ornament disconnected from serviceability.

## 11. Ink and edge language

### Relative line families — provisional

- **1.00:** outer silhouette of Varek, major machinery, support stone, and
  load-bearing timber.
- **0.55–0.70:** plates, joints, pipes, tools, doors, desks, and structural
  transitions.
- **0.30–0.45:** faces, hands, respirators, debt bands, seals, ledger marks,
  cloth folds, and small evidence.
- **Broken or absent:** smoke, deep void, glare, dust, distant galleries, and
  eroded stone.

The ratios scale with output resolution. They are not pixel widths until the
print profile is measured.

### Edge priority

- Varek's external silhouette is stable and engineered.
- Human faces and hands remain precise enough to carry hesitation and fatigue.
- Stone and cloth use broken internal edges.
- Fine machinery receives detail only where it clarifies function or evidence.
- Background industrial density may use depth/normal edge extraction, but its
  line weight must remain below the decision plane.

Uniform outline thickness is prohibited.

### Noise

Any Grease Pencil jitter is subtle, seeded, and applied by line family. It must
suggest physical ink without making structural steel look soft or unstable.

## 12. Tone, halftone, and texture

- Tonal classification precedes halftone.
- Halftone is screen-space and bound to the panel, not projected separately
  through every material.
- Midtones carry dots, hatch, soot, or paper tooth.
- Deep structural dark may remain solid.
- Faces receive less texture than clothing and walls unless illness, soot, or
  injury is the actual subject.
- Smoke may take coarse grain; it must not produce digital shimmer or moiré.
- Texture density decreases around the focal light.

The first proof page must calibrate dot frequency, angle, line ratios, and
downsample behavior. Until then, these settings remain `PROVISIONAL`.

## 13. Varek

### Locked read

Brothar Varek Fairgunjis is a mortal human permanently enclosed inside a
2.4384 m structural rescue and load-bearing machine with an approximately
three-tonne presence. He is not a robot, autonomous mech, superhero, knight,
cyborg god, or enlarged anatomical man.

The silhouette must preserve:

- protective operator-cell head language;
- dominant yoke-to-ground load path;
- external thoracic load cage around a smaller human body;
- integrated spinal diesel-hydraulic powerplant;
- pressure-vessel torso with hoop iron and rivets;
- broad planted leg frames and Anchor State ground interfaces;
- asymmetric, handmade Gren-Skildus history on character-left;
- separate 30 kg Fault Maul in the left hand, head-down when carried;
- right-arm Faírg-Tygil terminating in hydraulic talons around an open copper
  induction ring, not a conventional human hand.

### Emotional read

Varek should enter a room as a logistical problem:

- the doorway is too low;
- the floor must bear him;
- the steward's papers must be protected from his exhaust and mass;
- ordinary furniture becomes fragile;
- his voice and gestures remain careful.

His strongest visual contradiction is not man versus machine. It is enormous
capacity constrained by gentleness.

### Pose law

Prefer:

- stooping under architecture;
- turning sideways through a doorway;
- facing and protecting a load;
- bracing through hips and feet;
- shielding another person;
- holding still so someone smaller can work safely near him.

Reject:

- symmetrical hero stance;
- weapon presentation pose;
- chest-forward power fantasy;
- floating or weightless foot placement;
- tiny head inside a generic robot torso;
- giant pauldrons, glowing chest core, or ornamental military armor;
- active Faírg-Tygil loads without visible reaction through the suit and ground.

No page may imply a validated functional maul grip or load-bearing action while
the corresponding physics claim remains unauthorized.

## 14. Ordinary Thulans

People must look adapted to work, scarcity, and institutional classification,
not dressed in matching faction costumes.

### Shared visual language

- layered linen, wool, hide, and repaired work cloth;
- localized soot and mineral dust based on occupation;
- brass-canister respirators where air conditions require them;
- flip-up slag visors, ear protection, gloves, aprons, wraps, and tool belts;
- lead bands, tally marks, ration tags, guild stamps, mourning repairs, and
  inherited garments;
- bodies shaped by repetitive work, poor air, old injuries, and uneven food.

### Individuality

Differentiate people through:

- repair habits;
- occupational wear;
- how carefully they preserve one personal object;
- posture around authority;
- respiratory strain;
- family alterations to issued clothing;
- what they carry when ordered to leave.

Do not make poverty visually uniform. Unequal access to clean cloth, intact
filters, good metal, dry rooms, and warm light is part of the hierarchy.

## 15. Architecture and environment

### Built logic

Thulan spaces are excavated, shored, repaired, subdivided, and repurposed. They
are not pristine underground cathedrals.

Every important room should reveal:

- what physical work happens there;
- what keeps it ventilated, drained, lit, and supported;
- who controls entry;
- who receives the safe side of the room;
- what failed and was repaired;
- what evidence of prior occupants remains.

### Scale order

Human → Varek → machine → chamber → shaft → Mountain.

Do not skip directly from human to Mountain. Intermediate structures make the
scale credible.

### Vertical anxiety

Use:

- lift cages and counterweights;
- ladders disappearing beyond light;
- stacked doors and galleries;
- water stains descending through occupied tiers;
- hanging loads and overhead machinery;
- floor grates revealing lower labor;
- sound and smoke arriving from unseen levels.

Verticality should affect access, labor, air, rationing, and danger. It is not
background decoration.

### Institutional spaces

Records cells, ration rooms, tool cages, infirmaries, tithe chambers, and audit
desks should be more threatening through procedure than ornament. Repetition,
queues, numbered hooks, narrow counters, stamped hardware, and controlled light
carry the authority.

## 16. Evidence and consequence memory

Every sequence introduces, changes, or reactivates a physical evidence token.

Examples:

- another strip hammered onto a lead band;
- a filter stamped as unpaid equipment;
- a tool repaired with the wrong alloy because the correct stock is withheld;
- a ledger correction made in different ink;
- a ration hook retaining a dead worker's number;
- a survey mark older than the announced miracle;
- a brace left permanently bowed after a rescue;
- soot outlining where a confiscated family object used to hang.

Evidence persists across panels and later pages. A wound changes posture. A
broken tool changes work. A dead person's space does not reset.

## 17. Violence, injury, and gore

Gore is permitted when it proves force, danger, institutional neglect, or
irreversible cost. It is never decorative seasoning.

Show consequence through:

- compression and crush patterns;
- embedded grit and torn work cloth;
- blood mixing with mineral water, soot, or hydraulic fluid;
- missing protection or failed equipment;
- changed behavior by witnesses;
- cleanup, documentation, reassignment, and the empty place afterward.

Do not aestheticize suffering into a red accent. If injury does not change the
scene, the institution, or a person, remove it.

## 18. Lettering and graphic design

- Render images without baked-in dialogue, captions, or invented legible text.
- Reserve balloon and caption space in the thumbnail.
- Add lettering in the deterministic SVG page layer.
- Use rectangular institutional captions for records, decrees, and audit
  language; distinguish them from narration.
- Keep dialogue sparse, tired, work-specific, and physically immediate.
- Do not use decorative pseudo-runes. Existing white Gren-Skildus glyphs are
  rejected placeholders under RULING 011.
- The final font family and commercial license remain deferred.

## 19. Panel rejection gate

A panel is rejected before human art review if any answer is `NO`:

1. Is the human decision readable before the surface detail?
2. Does the value structure survive at thumbnail size?
3. Is the focal light motivated?
4. Does every major machine part have a visible purpose?
5. Does Varek read as a human in a rescue machine rather than a robot?
6. Are head, hands, and ground contact visible when required by the action?
7. Is institutional pressure expressed through physical evidence?
8. Does wear follow contact, heat, leakage, repair, or occupation?
9. Is the action geography understandable without dialogue?
10. Is the accent color carrying information?
11. Does the panel avoid unverified mechanical claims?
12. Could this image only belong to the Thulans?

Machine checks may reject a panel. They may not grant final visual approval.

## 20. Human review scorecard

Aaron reviews the exact hash-bound artifact on a 0–2 scale:

| Axis | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human anchor | absent | present but weak | immediate and emotionally specific |
| Thulan identity | generic genre image | partial | unmistakable without labels |
| Varek humanity | robot or hero | mixed | burdened human protector |
| Value read | muddy | readable | decisive hierarchy |
| Spatial truth | confusing | adequate | action and load path are clear |
| Evidence token | decorative or absent | present | changes understanding |
| Material history | random noise | plausible | specific service history |
| Institutional pressure | explained | implied | physically embodied |
| Restraint | overloaded | uneven | detail serves the decision |
| Consequence | resettable | acknowledged | persistent and costly |

No aggregate score can override a zero in **Thulan identity**, **Varek
humanity**, **spatial truth**, or **evidence token**. Those are stop conditions.

## 21. First proof-page art brief

### Page purpose

Prove that the pipeline can produce a distinctly Thulan page through restraint,
not spectacle.

### Panel 1 — Procedure

- Cramped records cell.
- Tired steward at an iron desk.
- One kerosene lamp creates the only warm pool.
- Damp stone, buckled ledgers, controlled shelving, cold corridor sliver.
- The steward's hand hesitates over a death record or survey date.
- The person is the subject. The room is the pressure.

### Panel 2 — Weight

- Varek enters through a low stone-and-iron threshold.
- He stoops and turns to protect the desk, paper, and steward from his own
  chassis and exhaust.
- Full silhouette, head, and ground contact remain visible.
- No heroic pose, active field effect, or functioning-grip claim.
- His scale is communicated through care.

### Panel 3 — Evidence

- Tight insert on the physical contradiction.
- Preferred subject: a survey slip predating the supposedly voluntary sacrifice,
  or a lead strip charging a survivor for the rescue.
- Fine human line, cold audit blue, one controlled amber reflection.
- The final image changes the reader's understanding without a lore lecture.

### Page palette

Charcoal and slate dominate. Bone parchment holds the focal information. Amber
appears only at the lamp and evidence reflection. Lead blue marks the system.
Gren-Skildus green may appear as a worn peripheral family note, not a focal
special effect.

### Proof-page failure condition

If the page mainly proves that the renderer can make attractive machinery, the
page fails.

## 22. Calibration still required

The following remain provisional until one proof panel is rendered, printed or
viewed at target size, downsampled, and rerun:

- exact line widths;
- halftone frequency and angle;
- tone thresholds;
- EEVEE sample profile;
- noise amplitude;
- print size, bleed, and color profile;
- balloon stroke and lettering sizes;
- camera lens values for the frozen scene;
- density limit for background edge extraction.

Record measured settings in the panel manifest. Do not promote this list into
fixed numbers by intuition.
