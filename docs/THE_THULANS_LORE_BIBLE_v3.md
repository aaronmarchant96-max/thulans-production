## 0. Naming & Linguistic Standard (Governing)

> Personal names should preferentially derive from attested Gothic names or securely
> grounded Gothic roots while remaining readable to an English-speaking audience.
> Fictional linguistic evolution is permitted, but invented forms must be labeled as
> such and must not be presented as historically attested Gothic.

### Tier ladder

| Tier | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **T1 — Attested** | A real, attested Gothic name or word used as-is. | *Wulfila*, *Fritigern* |
| **T2 — Adapted** | An attested Gothic root or name lightly smoothed for modern eyes. | *Haila* ← *hails*; *Watona* ← *wato* |
| **T3 — Invented** | A new descendant form built from real Gothic roots, explicitly labeled as a fictional evolution. | *Garuni* (formal cast of *garuni* "counsel / plan") |
| **T4 — Modern Drift** | A name in the modern lower-drift vernacular, drifted from attested Gothic or absorbed from contact dialects over 3,000 years. Permitted for modern characters only; it is *not* presented as attested Gothic and carries no classical root claim. | *Sanna*, *Bram*, *Kaelen*, *Malakor*, *Jarek*, *Tomas*, *Halza* |

### Conventions

- **No generic fantasy surnames.** *Ashwake, Blackstone, Ironborn, Emberfall* are
  prohibited unless a specific cultural reason exists.
- **Ordinary address:** `[Name] of [Hearth/place]`, e.g. *Wulfila of Azg-Haíms*.
- **Formal/ceremonial register:** the genitive **-jis** pattern already used by the IP
  (`Fairgunjis` = "of the Mountain", from attested *fairguni*) extends to dynastic
  titles. Example: *Wulfila Azghaimis*.
- **Patronymic / kinship grammar:** recorded in the companion `docs/THULAN_LANGUAGE_NOTE.md`
  (the *Giba-Broþrans* kinship frame — `broþar`, `swistar` — is locked).
- **Modern-drift names (T4):** modern characters carry lower-drift vernacular names
  drifted from attested Gothic or absorbed from contact dialects (e.g. *Sanna*, *Bram*,
  *Kaelen*, *Malakor*, *Jarek*, *Tomas*, *Halza*). They are permitted without classical
  root claims and are not to be presented as attested Gothic. See `THULAN_LANGUAGE_NOTE.md`.
- **Provenance labels:** every T2/T3 form carries a one-line root note so the repo's
  hash-binding rigor extends to language.

## 1. The Core Identity & World Pitch

> **"Brothar Varek Fairgunjis, Thulan of the Old Hearths."**

- **The Full Ceremonial Title:** **Brothar Varek Fairgunjis, Thulan of the Old Hearths**
  - **Brothar** (*Gothic: Broþar*): Not an ecclesiastical title, but the solemn
    brotherhood of the *Giba-Broþrans* and shared mortal kinship (*"We get each other
    home"*).
  - **Fairgunjis** (*Gothic Genitive*): "Of the Mountain" / "From the Mountain."
  - **Thulan of the Old Hearths:** Rooted in the ancient, uncorrupted era when
    protection and rescue were sacred duties, long before the modern regime's
    industrial exploitation.
- **The Thematic Irony:** He carries the Mountain in his name, his culture, and his
  ancestral duty—only to awaken three hundred years later and discover that the civilization
  built around it has consumed it almost completely from the inside.
- **The Setting:** A civilization surviving across and beneath a continent-sized
  Megalithic Mountain (*Fairguni*).
- **The Governing Tragedy:** The Thulans were not created by modern exploiters; they
  were engineered thousands of years ago when protection was still sacred. In the
  dying modern era, their symbols, mottoes, and memories have been twisted into state
  industrial propaganda to force exhausted miners to dig while the Mountain empties
  and its structure fails.
- **The Protagonist's Weight:** Varek is not a revolutionary demagogue. He becomes
  politically explosive because he remembers what the Mountain once was and recognizes
  that the modern "miracles" cannot be what the state claims.
- **The Central Tragedy:** Varek exposes the truth in the hope that people will finally
  be free to choose. Instead, he removes the political and religious support holding a
  dying civilization together and helps trigger its immediate collapse.

> He was right about the lie. He was wrong about what truth would do.

## 2. The Supreme Creative Guardrail: The Intimacy Rule

> **THE INTIMACY RULE**
>
> No faction, technology, religion, conflict, or historical event should exist only to
> make the setting larger. Every major element must connect to the lived cost of the
> Mountain's vertical hierarchy, the people trapped within it, or Varek's memory of
> those he once endured beside.
>
> The world may be enormous. The story must remain personal.
>
> **"Never let the Mountain become bigger than the people crushed beneath it."**

### The 7 Diagnostic Questions for All Future Lore

Before any new element is made canon, it must answer these seven questions:

1. Who has to climb farther because of this?
2. Who works deeper because of this?
3. Who is left behind because of this?
4. Who benefits from it?
5. Who pays for it?
6. What would Varek remember when he sees it?
7. How would one of his *Giba-Broþrans* have experienced it?

### The Consent Principle

> He volunteered to a lie.
>
> Consent obtained through manufactured causality is exploitation wearing consent as a
> mask.

This principle sits beside the Intimacy Rule.

The modern system's deepest violation is not merely that it forces people to die. It
creates false material conditions, false divine causality, and false urgency so that
honorable people will sometimes choose death on their own.

- Their courage is real.
- Their consent is real in the narrow sense that no hand physically forces them forward.
- The premise on which that consent rests is manufactured.

The state harvests genuine virtue as raw material.

### A. The Mountain as the Universe

To almost everyone alive, the Mountain is the world. Generational isolation has made
the idea of an exterior horizon or open sky seem physically absurd:

- A person can travel upwards for weeks through bustling caverns, residential wards,
  and transit tunnels and remain entirely inside stone.
- Distance is measured in levels and elevator drops: **Up-Mountain**, **Down-Mountain**,
  **Coreward**, and **Rimward**.

> The poor dig downward.
> The powerful climb upward.

### B. The Hidden Material Truth

The Mountain is not spiritually "withholding."

It is physically exhausted.

- The oldest galleries are stripped.
- Rich seams are gone.
- Ore quality has declined for generations.
- Structural pillars have been cut thinner than safe limits.
- Ancient machinery has been cannibalized.
- Public monuments and old infrastructure have quietly become secondary material
  reserves.

The civilization has spent centuries eating its own god from the inside out.

The ruling system knows this.

Most of the population does not.

## 4. Foundation Question 1: Faith, Manufactured Miracles & The Life Tithe

> "The Mountain never demanded sacrifice. People taught other people to sacrifice
> themselves to it."
>
> "The oldest faith taught stewardship. The dying civilization converted stewardship
> into extraction."
>
> "Ancient faith: the strong sacrifice FOR the vulnerable. Modern faith: the vulnerable
> are sacrificed FOR the system."

```
[ STAGE 1: ANCIENT RECIPROCAL COVENANT ]
  • "The Mother Mountain shelters us because we shelter the Mountain.
     Take carefully, restore what you disturb, leave viable stone for those unborn."
  • "ENDURE FOR THE MOUNTAIN."
    Hardship has purpose and limits; the strong bear burden so the Hearth survives.
                              │
[ STAGE 2: EARLY DRIFT ]
  • "The Mother provides for those who honor her."
                              │
[ STAGE 3: THE INDUSTRIAL EXTRACTION AGE ]
  • "Labor honors the Mother."
                              │
[ STAGE 4: THE ERA OF DECLINE ]
  • "The Mother withholds because we have become unworthy."
                              │
[ STAGE 5: MANUFACTURED CAUSALITY & THE REVEAL RITE ]
  • "ENDURE THE MOUNTAIN."
    Whatever the stone inflicts, you must bear silently.
  • "Only blood and sacrifice can restore her favor. Ore requires life."
```

### A. The Semantic Corruption: "Endure FOR" vs "Endure THE"

- **Ancient:** "ENDURE FOR THE MOUNTAIN." — Hardship has purpose and limits; the strong
  bear the burden so the vulnerable survive.
- **Modern:** "ENDURE THE MOUNTAIN." — Suffering itself is made proof of faith;
  whatever horrors the dying Mountain inflicts, the weak must suffer silently.

### B. Manufactured Divine Causality — The Reveal Rite

The state does not simply preach faith. It engineers evidence that appears to prove the
faith true.

1. Geological surveyors identify a remaining ore seam, water cavern, fuel reserve, or
   salvageable ancient stockpile.
2. The telemetry is classified.
3. The priesthood announces that the Mother is withholding because the population has
   become unworthy.
4. Desperate districts are ordered into public penance, increased quotas, or a Life
   Tithe.
5. After the sacrifice, the already-known reserve is "revealed."
6. The population is told:

> "The Mother has answered our blood."

### C. The Life Tithe as a Dual-Use Weapon

The Life Tithe performs two functions at once.

- **Public function:** manufacture faith by making a known resource release appear to
  be a divine response to sacrifice.
- **Hidden function:** remove people who threaten the mechanism while laundering their
  deaths through sacred ritual.

A dissident is not always arrested. A surveyor who has seen too much is not always
disappeared. A clerk who notices dates do not align is not always openly executed.

They can be tithed.

Once inside the ritual, political liquidation becomes indistinguishable from devotion.

The public receives one story:

> "The Mountain received one life and returned many."

### D. The Sacrifice Pool

The Council does not normally select individual names.

That work belongs to a clerical-administrative apparatus beneath the Provision/Faith
seat — formally **the Wahlja** ("the Choosing"). In public it is responsible for vows,
penance, criminal redemption, memorial honors, and volunteer petitions. Internally it
maintains an eligibility pool.

Possible sources include:

- **True volunteers** — sincere believers who offer themselves for their Hearth.
- **Condemned criminals** — offered ritual redemption.
- **Debtors and ration dependents** — pressured by promises of provision, debt relief,
  or honor for surviving family.
- **Political dissidents** — reclassified as spiritually dangerous, obstructive, or
  providentially "called."
- **Knowledge risks** — surveyors, clerks, engineers, logisticians, or priests who have
  seen enough to expose the timing mechanism.

These categories are not publicly distinguished.

A murdered whistleblower and a sincere volunteer emerge from the same rite wearing the
same sacred meaning.

The Council can therefore say:

> "We did not choose her."

That is technically true.

It is also morally evasive.

They approved the criteria, the quota, the timing, and the machine that produced the
name.

### E. The Volunteer — Wulfila of Azg-Haíms

Wulfila is nineteen, born in Azg-Haíms, a lower-district Hearth whose water table has
been failing for years.

His father raised him on Old Hearth stories as family history rather than modern
scripture: *the strong bear the burden so the vulnerable survive.*

Wulfila does not volunteer because he is dragged, condemned, or broken.

He volunteers clear-eyed.

He has done the arithmetic in the way the culture taught him to do it: one life against
a district's thirst.

He tells his sister that this is the only choice he has ever been completely certain of.

The Provision apparatus does not need to recruit him.

It only needs to ensure that his petition is accepted at the exact moment the Watona
release has already been scheduled internally.

His sincerity is the raw material.

The timing is the crime.

Varek initially recognizes something ancient and honorable in Wulfila's choice.

He may even tell Wulfila's sister:

> "He was brave."

And mean it.

Only later does Varek discover that the Watona was surveyed and approved before Wulfila
ever volunteered.

The state did not merely take Wulfila's life.

It reverse-engineered the kind of person who would willingly give it.

> "Wulfila volunteered to a lie."

### F. Wulfila's Kin — Sanna (sister) and Haila (betrothed). The Living Throughlines

RULING 029 fixes the family core: **Wulfila (19)** is the older brother of **Sanna (18)**
and **Bram (13)**. **Haila** is Wulfila's betrothed — she braided the cord and tied the
iron hammer-ring to his neck on the tithe platform.

For the current novella, Bram is thirteen during the main action on 22 October and
turns fourteen on 30 October. The epilogue occurs six years later. Earlier `Bram (14)`
labels do not establish his age during the main action (RULING 029 age clarification).

- **Haila** carries the rite itself: the betrothal the state converted into a sacrifice.
  She works beside Sanna in the Lower Four nursery, refusing to let the family starve.
- **Sanna** is the surviving sister and the load-bearer of the aftermath. She runs the
  nursery, keeps Bram alive after his leg is crushed, and is the last person to hold
  Varek's hand as he dies (RULING 031). She is the human face of the fact that *truth is
  not the same thing as healing.*

Neither woman becomes a general, a chosen figure, or a replacement protagonist. They
remain ordinary people forced to live through both betrayals.

When Varek brings evidence that the aquifer was already known, he may begin, *"He was
brave,"* and one of them answers:

> "Then don't take this from him."
>
> Varek: "I'm not taking it from him. They did."

They are uniquely positioned to condemn both systems:

- To the Council: "You murdered him."
- To Varek, who stood silent on the platform as the state's ceremonial iron: "You were
  there."

Varek's answer is not a speech. It is his body: he tears his own rig apart to keep
Sanna's nursery pump running and dies holding her hand. The debt to Wulfila's family is
paid in iron, not in explanation.

After the collapse, the contest is no longer over what the truth was but over who
survives it. Sanna continues helping other people live — not because she forgives the
religion or Varek, but because the underlying virtue belonged to ordinary people before
the state stole it.

The Council corrupted sacrifice.

It did not invent human selflessness.

### G. Varek's Lethal Question

When a new "miracle" is proclaimed after another rite, Varek does not argue theology.

He asks:

> "When was it surveyed?"

That question becomes the hinge of the entire civilization.

## 5. Mountain Warfare Mechanics

Because space is vertical, military conflict in the Mountain follows unique industrial
rules:

1. **Ventilation Sieges:** Armies seize upper air-intake shafts and pump sulfur or
   carbon monoxide down-mountain to suffocate rival cities.
2. **Aquifer Diversions:** Reservoir gates are seized or destroyed to cut off water and
   force surrender.
3. **Foundation Undermining:** Rival districts excavate beneath load-bearing arches
   until entire neighborhoods shear into the depths.
4. **Elevator Bastions:** Fortified transit choke-points control movement between levels
   through counterweights, drop-gates, and lift monopolies.

### 5.1. The Welded (*Gathaur-Bano*) — Penal Actuator Frames

A Welded is a **work machine with a captive physically restrained in its moving work envelope**, not wearable armour or a miniature Varek. The rescue-engineering **ethical inversion** remains a development principle, but exact shared component geometry or direct manufacturing descent is **not established**. The **Chapter Six mobile breaker** must visibly match the written ground-striking bars, low mask, dragging foot, human hand below cuff, drive-pin break and tool-removable mask. Nessa's station gripper is a **distinct job machine**, not a weapon swapped onto the same heroic suit. [Visual Canon Reset V0.2](WELDED_VISUAL_CANON_RESET_V02.md) governs visual development; [Visual Lineage V0.1](WELDED_VISUAL_LINEAGE_V01.md) is superseded. No final character model has been approved.

**29 October breaker variant lock — [RULING 040](../RULINGS.md#ruling-040-welded-breaker-lock):** The author has now approved **one exact hulking mobile breaker reference** ([visual lock and image hash](WELDED_BREAKER_VISUAL_LOCK_V01.md)). Its patched boiler, low face-fitted mask, exposed living captive, heavy shoulders and single enormous driven breaker are approved as **the breaker form**, with the pictured numeral **9** explicitly a placeholder because Nessa's separate Frame 9 remains reserved. This supersedes RULING 039's "no image approved" statement **for that breaker only**. Nessa's station gripper remains open. Chapter Six now includes **Varek bending open the disabled chest cage and lifting the freed boy out**, while Kaelen cuts the internal restraints, removes the mask, and withdraws the feeding tube. The action of freeing both living men remains unchanged in outcome.

* **Sentence and purpose:** The Spire puts people it deems unfit for society into powered frames instead of prison. Theft, debt, quota offences, and insubordination can supply the justification. Sentenced people are used for compulsory dangerous work and deployed to suppress unrest and breach resistance. The prisoner's consciousness and cooperation are not required for the frame to act.
* **The concealed permanence:** A stated term creates the expectation of release, but nobody is released through ordinary sentence completion. Ansen and Nessa receive three months for stealing bread from the upper Spire; five years pass without release or an announced extension. Varek physically freeing prisoners is an exception to the confinement, not a functioning appeal process. The exact administrative concealment remains open.
* **Power and control:** Steam supplies the force, with pumps, valves, hydraulic actuators, and mechanical drives transmitting movement. Handlers direct a limited set of imposed actions through external controls; an engaged drive can keep cycling until stopped or disabled. Movement builds pressure, jerks against stops, and vents. No telepathy or unexplained voluntary obedience is required. Exact command hardware and control-line routing remain to be designed.
* **Human restraint:** The frame carries limbs in cuffs, braces, and harnesses. A person can look, speak with difficulty, and struggle in small ways, but cannot overpower the powered movement or reach the normal controls. Resistance and refusal of consent remain possible; simply deciding not to strike does not disengage the machinery.
* **Mask and feeding apparatus:** A riveted mask leaves narrow sight-slits. Long-term feeding tubes and tight restraints cause the manuscript's lip notch, pressure sores, and damaged skin. Speech remains possible in short, painful phrases. The former coal-naphtha/adrenaline mouth-drip explanation is superseded for this literary continuity; no stimulant regimen is newly established.
* **Breaker variant — written and visually locked:** Chapter Six's rigs advance under power with one huge breaker arm per rig; the back boiler/cam can continue cycling while a prisoner resists. Varek shears the first breaker drive pin, catches the chest frame, shuts steam and vents the pump. Later, Varek bends the disabled chest cage open while Kaelen releases straps, faceplate and feeding line; Varek lifts the injured boy free. Varek breaks the second rig's drive wheel/chain, and both prisoners survive. Preserve the first miner's broken wrist and the approved breaker silhouette under RULING 040.
* **Gripping variant — approved development:** Nessa is confined in an enforcement frame whose powered gauntlet imposes a seizure. A handler's command forces it around Ansen's throat while she tries to resist. Loaded coupling pins, arm travel, hand linkage, force, and accessible shutoff must be made legible in the final design. This is a narrative mechanism to develop, not a tested engineering claim.
* **Sound:** Wheezing breaths, forced words, iron footfalls, valve clicks, and relief venting carry the people beneath the mechanism. Avoid monster roars and theatrical battle cries.
* **Human consequence:** The occupants are people subjected to violence, not willing monsters. Rescue can succeed, fail, or arrive too late. Do not prescribe killing as mercy for every encounter or erase the rescued prisoners' continuing lives.

    collar of the Fault Maul (*Faírguni-Hamars*). True strength is having the sovereign power
    to choose what you stand between; a direct philosophical counter to the Council's coerced
    Life Tithe.
  - **`ORA ET LABORA` ("Pray and Work"):** Stamped into an inner structural plate behind
    the curve of the Gren-Skildus near his chest. *"Reverence does not replace labor."* Before
    dangerous structural drops, Varek instinctively brushes the inner plate with his gauntlet:
    > *“Ora et labora.”*  
    Then the diesel catches, hydraulics pressurize, and he goes to work.

- **The Load-Coupler (*Faírg-Tygil*) & Grounded Physics (RULING 024, RULING 030):** The
  right-arm manipulator grips ferromagnetic ore bands and iron sills through high-flux
  pulsed electromagnetism, transferring reaction load down the arm into the chassis and
  anchored boots. It never negates mass or cancels gravity; *"graviton"* survives only as
  lower-drift slang. Shaft 44's rock is **banded magnetite-ironstone** (*blôþ-stáin*),
  not non-magnetic basalt.

**Living Payoff:**

> "It's not a man. It's an ancient machine."
> *(Hiss of the respirator)*
> "I had a mother."

## 7. The Empty Mountain — The Primary Revelation

The central revelation is not supernatural.

It is material.

Varek reaches one of the oldest sealed strata and recognizes where an ancient productive
seam should be.

The state claims the region is sacred, unstable, forbidden, exhausted only because the
Mother withdrew her favor.

Varek knows the old workings.

He opens them.

And there is almost nothing left.

Not one failed mine.

Not one bad district.

The Mountain itself has been systematically stripped.

- Miles of dead galleries.
- Removed structural ribs.
- Recycled supports.
- Cannibalized sacred machinery.
- Old metal recast and presented as newly blessed ore.
- Survey markers showing reserves were known years before their supposed "miraculous"
  discovery.

The civilization has not been waiting for the Mountain to provide.

It has been rationing the last pieces of a corpse.

Varek realizes immediately what this means:

> There is nothing left to withhold.

## 8. The Second Revelation — The Sacrifices Were Orchestrated

The emptiness alone is catastrophic.

The survey chronology is worse.

Records, markers, dispatch orders, lift logs, and technical testimony begin to align.

A pattern becomes undeniable:

```
reserve discovered
      ↓
survey classified
      ↓
public shortage allowed to worsen
      ↓
priesthood declares divine displeasure
      ↓
Life Tithe / penance / labor sacrifice
      ↓
known reserve "revealed"
      ↓
faith strengthened
      ↓
production restored
```

The population realizes that the state did not merely exploit religious belief.

It manufactured divine causality.

The people sacrificed were not buying salvation.

The resource was already there.

The sacrifice existed to make obedience feel causally necessary.

Some of the dead had been condemned.

Some had been politically inconvenient.

Some had discovered too much.

And some had truly volunteered.

That final category becomes the most morally devastating, because the regime did not have
to force them. It constructed the lie, waited for an honorable person to believe it, and
then accepted the life that belief produced.

### The social detonation

The realization spreads through technical workers first:

- surveyors compare dates;
- lift operators remember sealed shipments;
- refinery clerks recognize pre-rite inventory movements;
- priests discover liturgical orders timed to logistics;
- families compare the deaths of their loved ones with resource-release records.

Then the question spreads:

> "When was it surveyed?"

It becomes accusation, proof request, revolutionary slogan, and eventually a weapon.

### The Cold Well

After the Wulfila evidence begins moving through survey crews, shift boards, manifests
and Hearth networks, the proof acquires an oral form.

"The Cold Well" begins as a Lower-Hearth work chant and becomes the people's mnemonic
for the crime.

Varek's technical question—

> "When was it surveyed?"

—becomes:

> "Who knew? Who knew?  
> Say when you knew!"

The song matters because it makes the evidence impossible to contain through document
seizure alone. A ledger can be burned. Thousands of miners can remember a refrain.

Its final moral claim is not that the Mountain was never dying. It is that the people
were taught to mistake controlled access for divine response.

> "The Mountain did not answer us—  
> we answered for the lost."

See **RULING 021** for canonical lyric and musical authority.

## 9. Varek's Fatal Miscalculation

### The want he wakes with (RULING 018)

Varek does not wake up political. He wakes up searching.

He wakes believing something of his home survived — evidence that the civilization he
sacrificed for preserved something recognizable: a Hearth custom, a family line, his
rescue oath, a surviving district, a name carved into a wall.

Modern people fascinate him because they are proof that his people survived.

Then the horror resolves into something worse than future shock:

> They survived biologically.
> The moral world he remembered did not.

His cultural vertigo becomes extremely specific. Words he knows still exist but mean
different things. A Thulan was once the person who stayed so someone else could leave;
now endurance is a theology telling weaker people to stay in suffering. Sacrifice once
meant choosing to bear a burden; now the state assigns burdens and calls the result
sacred. His language survived. Its moral grammar was inverted.

That is why he lets himself look and listen and hope. He is not auditing a regime. He
is looking for home.

---

Varek exposes the truth because he believes people who know the truth can finally choose
for themselves.

His intention is liberation.

He believes:

> "If they know, they can choose."

He is morally right about the lie.

He is catastrophically wrong about what the truth will do to a civilization whose order
depends on that lie.

The revelation does not produce orderly freedom.

It destroys legitimacy almost instantly.

- The religion collapses.
- The state loses moral authority.
- Workers abandon critical shifts.
- Families seek vengeance.
- Old caste grievances erupt.
- Districts stop recognizing central commands.
- Supply chains fracture.
- Local militias seize lifts, aquifers, furnaces, ventilation shafts, and ration stores.
- Upper levels barricade themselves.
- Lower levels lose heat and air.

People who spent generations believing their dead saved the community discover that
those deaths were staged political theater.

Some turn on the state.

Some turn on the priesthood.

Some turn on Varek.

Some refuse to believe him because accepting the truth would mean accepting that their
child, spouse, parent, or sibling died for nothing.

### Varek's realization

He had spent his life learning never to remove a structural support without first
understanding what it carried.

Politically, he did exactly that.

> "I thought I was opening a door."
>
> "I was pulling a support."

The lie was monstrous.

The lie was also load-bearing.

Both are true.

## 10. The Immediate Collapse

The civilization was already dying slowly.

Varek's revelation converts slow decline into rapid failure.

It takes **weeks, not days**. Evidence moves through surveyors, shift boards,
manifests, guild presses, and ordinary logistics before the collapse becomes general.
Then:

- mine shifts disappear;
- furnace output crashes;
- ventilation maintenance becomes irregular;
- lift operators abandon contested routes;
- ration depots are looted;
- priests are murdered or flee;
- some clergy die defending communities they genuinely believed they served;
- regional authorities declare independence;
- industrial guilds become armed governments;
- aquifers are seized;
- ventilation systems become siege weapons;
- old blood feuds return;
- bridges and support systems go unmaintained;
- entire lower districts lose breathable air;
- collapse propagates through infrastructure that had no redundancy left.

The revelation did not create the rot.

It removed the structure concealing and containing it.

Varek hoped to set them free.

Instead, he condemned them to anarchy before they had any structure capable of surviving
freedom.

## 11. The Council of Three — *Garuni*

The ruling system's central human opposition is not one villain.

It is a Council of Three positioned between physical resource reality and the population
that depends on it: **Garuni** ("the Counsel").

They are not necessarily the highest ceremonial rulers in the civilization.

They are the operational hinge that decides what the Mountain is permitted to "reveal."

### Seat One — Survey / Reserve — *Fritigern*

This seat knows:

- what resources remain;
- where they are;
- when they were discovered;
- projected depletion curves;
- hidden stockpile levels;
- which reserves are technically recoverable;
- how much of the Mountain is already dead.

Their sin is controlled knowledge.

They decide what geological truth remains classified.

The name *Fritigern* ("craving peace") is deliberate irony: this is the seat that chose
concealment as its peace.

### Seat Two — Continuity / Infrastructure — *Theodemir*

This seat knows what must keep functioning:

- ventilation;
- water;
- lifts;
- furnace heat;
- structural stabilization;
- fuel;
- emergency transport;
- district dependency chains.

Their worldview is brutally mathematical.

A failed pump is not symbolic.

It kills people.

A missed maintenance shift can suffocate a district.

This seat determines how long a reserve can be withheld before system failure becomes
unacceptable.

### Seat Three — Provision / Faith — *Amalasuntha*

This seat controls how scarcity is translated into public meaning:

- ration policy;
- district priority;
- priesthood coordination;
- Reveal Rite timing;
- public penance;
- Life Tithe quotas;
- the Wahlja — the devotional bureaucracy that selects individual candidates.

This is where logistics become theology.

Amalasuntha is a woman; the irony is deliberate. The faith she administers worships the
Mother, and the seat that feeds the Mother with names is held by a ruthless mother of
the state.

### The Ministries and the Order Directorate

The three seats are administered as ministries, and the prose uses their public names
interchangeably with the seat names:

- **Ministry of Continuity** — the public name of Seat Two's apparatus. It also houses
  the **Ministry of Order**: a non-voting enforcement directorate, **not** a fourth
  chair. Order commands the pneumatic-augment squads and black-pneumatic-brace enforcers
  and enforces production quotas across the drifts. It answers to Theodemir's slates.
- **Ministry of Faith** — the public name of Seat Three's apparatus, covering the
  deified Fairguna religion, the school catechisms, and the ceremonial Life Tithes.
  "Ministry of Faith" and "Provision/Faith seat" refer to the same institution.

The Council therefore has three voting seats and one subordinate enforcement arm. The
Faith apparatus supplies meaning; the Order directorate supplies teeth; both serve the
production arithmetic of Continuity.

### The Closed Loop

A typical decision can look like this:

> **Survey/Reserve:** "The Watona is viable."
>
> **Continuity/Infrastructure:** "Releasing it now buys six months. We cannot afford
> uncontrolled consumption."
>
> **Provision/Faith:** "Then access must appear earned."

The Council does not need to say:

> "Kill Wulfila."

It can say:

> "One Soul-Tenth before release."

The Wahlja produces the person.

That distance is the horror.

### The Council's Moral Defense

They argue:

- the Mountain is materially exhausted;
- the remaining reserves are finite;
- public knowledge would trigger immediate seizure and hoarding;
- uncontrolled districts would fight over water, air, and heat;
- labor discipline would collapse;
- infrastructure failure would kill far more people than the rites;
- therefore managed deception extends civilization's remaining life.

They can tell Varek:

> "You remember a world that could afford your morality."

And later, after the collapse begins:

> "You gave them truth. What will they breathe tomorrow?"

The regime's crimes remain real.

So does the fact that the collapse they predicted actually happens.

### The Hidden Clock

The Council's defense is partially honest and partially the crime.

The timescale Seat Two is actually defending is not the civilization's remaining life
alone: it is the **ark-launch clock**. The same continuity that keeps pumps and furnaces
running also keeps production and social order intact until the exodus arks are ready to
depart. Continuity does not lie to the population about the Mountain's emptiness — the
sleeping truth of the arks is the clause the defense never states aloud.

They kept people breathing.

And they were winding a clock that would leave most of them behind.

### Storycraft directive — guidance for execution, not canon

The Council must never be narratively absolved by saying *"we approved criteria, we did
not choose names."*

The story should repeatedly place abstraction beside consequence. A Council scene may
contain nothing more emotional than a release ratio, a depletion curve, a district
viability table, and "one Tithe per major release."

The story should then cut to the body that satisfies that line item.

The distance between the spreadsheet and the corpse is part of the crime.

No Council member needs to enjoy the system.

All three are still responsible for maintaining it.

### Internal Division

The three should not have identical consciences.

- One may hate the Life Tithes but believe stopping them would collapse critical systems.
- One may believe ritual suffering is an ugly but mathematically defensible tool.
- One may have spent so long preserving continuity that survival of the population,
  survival of the state, and survival of their own authority have become
  indistinguishable.

When Varek begins asking "When was it surveyed?", they should disagree about him.

One wants containment.

One wants partial disclosure and recruitment.

One believes killing an ancient Thulan would itself destroy legitimacy.

That hesitation helps the truth escape.

### The Vertical Economy (RULING 016)

The Mountain's economy descends through four increasingly intimate forms of surrender:

> **Labor → entitlement → body → life**

Each step is a deeper claim the civilization makes upon the individual.

- **Hearth Marks** — ordinary exchange. Wages, rent, tools, repairs, trade, transport,
  clothing, services, goods. Marks are purchasing power; they do not guarantee access to
  survival resources. A person may have enough marks to buy water and still be unable to
  obtain it.
- **Provision entitlements** — water, staple food, heat, medical access, and other
  strategically scarce necessities require a valid entitlement in addition to payment.
  The defining contradiction: *having money does not mean having permission* — and
  permission does not mean the resource remains available. The system once had a
  legitimate purpose (an enclosed industrial civilization cannot allow unrestricted
  consumption of critical resources); its corruption begins when control over allocation
  becomes control over behavior.
- **Marrow contracts** — a secondary desperation market where clinics compensate people
  for biological material used in degraded regenerative medicine. The rationale is
  inherited ancient bioscience, preserved imperfectly and repeatedly rebuilt; its exact
  capabilities remain fictional and bounded. The Mountain's hazards create genuine demand
  (radiation, heavy metals, furnace chemistry, industrial toxins, contaminated dust,
  severe trauma, catastrophic blood loss). Recovery is possible but non-trivial: repeat
  sellers become recognizable — fatigue, bruising, reduced work capacity. The poverty
  loop: a parent sells marrow to buy a child water, then leaves less able to earn the
  next month's provisions. **Enter because you cannot afford survival. Leave less able
  to afford survival.** The Summit abstracts it (*"regenerative reserves"*,
  *"compatibility banks"*, *"VOLUNTARY REGENERATIVE CONTRIBUTION"*); the Hearths call it
  what it is — *selling marrow.* Institutional language removes the body from the
  transaction; Lower-Mountain speech puts it back.
- **Life Tithe** — sits beyond economics. Not the final commodity but the sanctified
  endpoint: duty, sacrifice, provision, honor, devotion, communal salvation. The state
  refuses to describe it as exchange, claiming the individual has transcended selfish
  economics. It is the same descent given holy language.

The Hearth's moral resistance: **"A body may be given. It should not have to be sold."**
Remembered sharply as **"No Hearth prices blood."** A principle, not a law — poorer
Hearths break it because principles do not fill an empty cistern. Donation honored;
coerced sale a communal failure.

Wulfila's district walked the entire ladder before the Tithe — overtime, sold tools,
spent marks, traded entitlements, took debt, sold marrow — and the cistern still ran dry.
Then the authorities said **"The Mountain requires more."** The Tithe did not appear as
cartoon evil; it appeared as the next entry in a ledger. Wulfila takes the logic seriously
enough to offer the final thing: his life. Because the aquifer was pre-surveyed, none of
it was necessary. The crime is not one dead volunteer but a district consumed down the
self-consumption ladder to make the final sacrifice believable.

> The Mountain is exhausted. The people are being taught to exhaust themselves with it.

## 13. End-State Principles and the Written Novella

**Scope clarification (RULING 034):** The older catastrophic macro-outcome statements below are planning history, not a newly approved finale for the rebellion novellas. The written ending remains the six-year domestic scene and Sanna's answer. Varek dies before the wider rebellion; the people he leaves behind carry its unresolved consequences.

There is no restoration of the old order.

There is no clean revolution.

There is no moment where everyone learns the truth, overthrows the rulers, and builds a
better society.

- The Mountain is still materially exhausted.
- Its infrastructure is still failing.
- Its supports are still compromised.
- Its people are now divided and armed.

The truth does not refill the mines.

Freedom does not restart the pumps.

Justice does not restore the dead.

Varek may save a district, a refuge, a Hearth, or a few thousand people.

He does not save the civilization.

The ending is not "civilization destroyed, therefore nothing mattered." The governing
end-state sentence (RULING 013):

> The Thulans does not end with restoration of the Mountain, the Council, or the old
> order. Its final answer is smaller. Truthful obligation returns to the Hearth: people
> choosing what they will bear for one another without manufactured necessity, sacred
> coercion, or promises that suffering will purchase salvation. The state may fail.
> Human responsibility does not.

Civilization fails at scale. Moral agency survives at human scale. The macro ending is
catastrophic; the micro ending has to contain life.

The ending should preserve one small human principle without pretending that principle
fixes the world.

Varek does not live to see the macro outcome. His story ends in Lower Hearth Four, when
he returns his iron to the hands that built it and dies holding Sanna's hand (RULING 031).
The older *"Will the Mountain protect us? / We will."* exchange — in which Varek was still
alive to speak it — is **superseded**; the survival of the principle is carried instead by
the people he leaves behind and by the residual-machine epilogue (§16).

The canonical final image is smaller and colder. Six years after the dismantling, a child
in Lower Four asks his mother whether the man on the bronze statue died for the Spire:

> "No," Sanna says. "It was our iron anyway."

Outside, the civilization is still collapsing.

That is not victory.

It is the oldest ethic surviving after the civilization built around it has failed.

> We get each other home.

And the final wound is that home may no longer exist.

The state may fail. Human responsibility does not.
