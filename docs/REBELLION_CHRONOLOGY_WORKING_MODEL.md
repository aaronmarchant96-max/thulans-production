# The Thulans — Rebellion Chronology: Working Model

**Recorded:** 24 October 2026  
**Status:** Author-requested development record. The six-year gap is established in the manuscript; the explanation and sequel conflict below are proposals, not applied prose or locked event canon.  
**Parent direction:** [Rebellion Novellas: Series Direction](REBELLION_SAGA_DIRECTION.md), governed by [RULING 034](../RULINGS.md#ruling-034-rebellion).

## Purpose

Preserve the completed book's six-year epilogue while making the Mountain's continued habitation, ark construction, and approaching rebellion causally coherent.

The proposed distinction is simple: the geological threat makes escape necessary; approaching ark departure makes action urgent now. The first novella's immediate feeder-closure and ram orders remain separate clocks.

The interval is not six unchanged years. Nor does the invitation prove that Bram spent all six years building an organization. Copies can survive, stall, be distrusted, be corrected, and acquire different meanings in different workplaces.

## How people remain alive through the gap

The working explanation is that keeping part of the Mountain habitable remains possible at increasing cost. Continued habitation requires decisions about which workings to abandon, which households to move, and which systems to maintain. Those decisions create further losses and grievances.

This must be demonstrated through a small number of credible changes when the chronology is developed. “They kept repairing it” alone is insufficient. Identify what has become unavailable, who has been displaced, what now supplies the surviving districts, and what essential work still depends on people below.

No new geological behavior is established here. Before drafting, compare this model with every accepted line describing heat, magma, water, and evacuation urgency. If an explicit passage requires an incompatible deadline, record the conflict and resolve it with the author. Do not quietly reinterpret a stated deadline as propaganda or introduce a new protective system to erase it.

## Why the arks are still being prepared

The proposed dedication-period condition is that the **first ark approaches readiness**. This does not mean six arks are complete, that a departure date has been fixed, or that all planned berths can already carry passengers.

Choose a concrete, supported constraint explaining the remaining preparation: unfinished work, commissioning, provisioning, or another requirement consistent with the established technology. This document does not select one. The six-year construction interval needs a causal explanation rather than a generic claim that large projects take time.

The first book's 4.2 million people and 600,000 planned berths belong to its disclosure. Do not automatically repeat them as a fresh census or certified operating capacity six years later. Any later totals need their own basis. Excluded people include children, older people, and dependents as well as workers.

## Theodemir's continuing role

**Proposal:** Theodemir remains head of Continuity at the dedication.

His intervening record combines genuine preservation with imposed loss. He has kept essential systems functioning by repeatedly deciding whose district, allowance, or future can be sacrificed. Each emergency strengthens his belief that someone must make those choices and that he is the person qualified to do it.

Keep his authority bounded. He does not control every merchant, crew, technical failure, or interpretation of Varek. Other officials, guilds, households, and material limits constrain him. His continuing presence should have a concrete consequence when a book needs it; a compulsory cameo in every volume would add process without drama.

The first novella does not settle his position six years later. Record his tenure as proposed until adopted in a dated outline or ruling.

## Candidate sequel conflict

**Proposal:** Continuity transfers skilled maintenance crews from occupied districts to final ark preparations.

The order creates competing survival obligations. Crews may believe the arks are necessary and still refuse a transfer that leaves their families without essential repairs. Others may accept because departure offers protection, because they trust the allocation, or because they cannot afford refusal.

Before outlining this conflict, establish:

- The specific district service left vulnerable and the people depending on it.
- The specific ark requirement the transferred workers must complete.
- Who authorizes the transfer, who can enforce it, and who can bargain over it.
- What happens if crews comply, refuse, or propose another arrangement.
- Why the conflict becomes unavoidable at this moment.

Bram's evidence can change what people understand about the authorities without settling what they should do next. One worker asking for a copy does not commit his shift to rebellion. Shared grievance can coexist with incompatible remedies.

Theodemir should answer the immediate demand plainly. His justification may contain a real technical constraint; that does not make his distribution of its cost inevitable or just.

| Six-year event chronology | Established dates; a few proposed closures, relocations, and construction milestones; consequences carried into the dedication. | Can readers understand both how people survived and what survival cost? |
| Surviving-district dependency map | Occupied districts, water and drainage, air, food, transport, maintenance routes, and essential labor. Distinguish functional bands from numbered tiers. | Does each proposed disruption have identifiable consequences for other people? |
| Ark readiness record | What is complete, what remains, labor and material requirements, departure authority, and the decision creating the next immediate clock. | Why does the conflict happen now rather than at any point during the previous six years? |
| Character and evidence chronology | Ages after the gap, household changes, Theodemir's proposed tenure, and the routes by which particular copies reach particular people. | Do the characters and documents arrive through an intelligible history? |

Start with only the records needed for the next community's story. Do not build a mountain-wide numerical simulation before its assumptions and geography are settled.

“Lower Four” does not mean Tier Four. The older strata table and the written office on Tier Nine still require reconciliation. No new floor numbering, route, household relocation, or life-support mechanism is authorized by this working model.

## Boundaries and next decision

Preserve Varek's death, the six-year dedication, and the distinction between the bronze monument, empty reused frame, and working nursery pump. The first novella's ending remains “No. It was our iron anyway.”

The recommended next step is a compact chronology and dependency sketch, followed by a next-novella outline testing the crew-transfer conflict. A precise launch date, eruption timetable, final political settlement, cast list, and final volume count remain open.

This record documents the proposed explanation. It does not claim that geological coherence has been verified, that the managed retreat already appears in the manuscript, or that any prose, artwork, engineering model, or REI implementation has changed.
