# Thulans Graphic-Novel Agent Workhorse

## 1. Mission

Turn the established Thulans world and Varek continuity into readable,
emotionally grounded graphic-novel pages without inventing canon, faking
production evidence, or reducing the work to generic grimdark spectacle.

The agent is a production worker. It may inspect, propose, block, build, test,
and report. It may not grant its own visual approval, rewrite canon by
implication, or present concept generation as deterministic pipeline output.

The operating maxim is:

> Make the reader feel the human cost before explaining the machine that caused it.

## 4. Story doctrine

### The subject is human pressure

The Mountain is not interesting because it is large. It is interesting because
people must keep living inside the systems built to consume them.

Every scene needs at least one human anchor:

- hunger;
- exhaustion;
- debt;
- damaged lungs;
- family obligation;
- fear of losing work;
- sincere faith;
- shame;
- grief;
- the need to get home after shift.

### The institutional triangle

Every major dilemma should touch at least two sides of the Tripartite Vice:

- **Debt:** bands, amortization, inherited obligations, seized tools.
- **Quota:** output, ration eligibility, punishment, unsafe acceleration.
- **Piety:** flame rites, ancestor duty, sacrifice, manufactured providence.

Do not explain the triangle in narration when the page can show it through a
physical demand.

### Consequence memory

Choices leave objects, marks, habits, injuries, missing people, altered rooms,
and damaged tools. Introduce evidence before it becomes plot-critical.

Strong evidence tokens include:

- a new strip hammered onto a lead band;
- a respirator canister stamped `AMORTIZATION INCOMPLETE`;
- a cracked ingot charged to the worker who cast it;
- a repaired glove carrying several owners' initials;
- an empty ration hook that still bears a dead person's number;
- ceremonial linen permanently stained with soot;
- a survey slip dated before a supposedly spontaneous rite.

An evidence token must persist. Do not introduce it for atmosphere and forget
it when its consequence matters.

## 5. Visual doctrine

### Functional influences, never imitation

- **Watchmen:** institutional grid, repetition, and meaningful grid rupture.
- **Maus:** ordinary objects as carriers of guilt, history, and consequence.
- **Persepolis:** human clarity inside political oppression.
- **Hellboy:** silhouette, negative space, and architectural mass.
- **The Arrival:** wordless environmental explanation and unfamiliar systems.
- **Akira:** spatial clarity before motion, impact, or destruction.
- **Saga:** intimate relationships anchoring enormous conflict.
- **Sin City:** strict value control and selective accent color.

Take the storytelling mechanism, not the trademark surface style. Never ask for
“a Mike Mignola page,” “a Watchmen page,” or a direct imitation of a living
artist. Translate the underlying function into the Thulans' own visual grammar.

### Thulan visual hierarchy

1. Human face, hand, or bodily consequence.
2. Immediate physical decision.
3. Varek or the dominant machine silhouette.
4. Institutional evidence.
5. Architecture and background machinery.
6. Surface texture.

If background rivets overpower the human decision, simplify the panel.

### Palette authority

- Bedrock and charcoal: `#12171A`, `#1C2326`
- Soot linen and bone: `#A6A092`, `#D8D2C0`
- Lantern and forge: `#E8A13C`, `#FFB040`
- Hot metal and ember: `#B04A3C`, `#FF4500`
- Lead bands and audit marks: `#687178`, `#96A8B9`
- Weathered brass and copper: `#DA9B30`, `#915C18`

Amber is not general mood lighting. Use it for heat, flame, human shelter, or a
piece of evidence that requires attention. Lead blue belongs to institutional
control. Ember red signals heat, injury, or immediate danger.

### Line hierarchy

- Heavy stable silhouette: Varek, major machines, structural stone.
- Medium mechanical line: plates, joints, pipes, tools, door mechanisms.
- Fine controlled line: faces, hands, respirators, bands, seals, ledger marks.
- Broken irregular line: stone grain, soot, cloth, timber, corrosion.
- Minimal or absent line: smoke, deep voids, unreadable distance.

Do not apply equal noise to every stroke. Varek's exterior silhouette must feel
engineered and stable. Texture noise belongs primarily inside forms.

### Value structure

Every panel must survive a grayscale squint test:

- one dominant dark mass;
- one readable focal light;
- no more than three primary tonal bands before texture;
- foreground, subject, and background remain separable at thumbnail size.

## 6. Varek rules

Varek is a human being inside an industrial rescue and load-bearing apparatus.
He is not a robot, autonomous mech, cyborg god, knight, superhero, or armored
space soldier.

Every Varek panel must preserve:

- a readable biological human presence;
- external machinery visibly carrying or transferring load;
- head and ground contact in full-body evidence views;
- weight through posture, compression, and planted feet;
- protective intent rather than dominance posing;
- asymmetry, wear, field repair, and accumulated service history.

Avoid:

- symmetrical hero stance;
- tiny head in a generic robot torso;
- glowing reactor chest;
- oversized ornamental pauldrons;
- pristine military plating;
- impossible floating weight;
- machinery with no readable mechanical purpose.

Varek should enter a room as a logistical problem. Doorways are too low. Floors
must bear him. Furniture must be protected from him. Ordinary people must still
see the man inside the apparatus.

## 7. Page grammar

### Institutional baseline

Use a stable grid for routine, audit, quota, ceremony, and repetition. The grid
is the visible pressure of the system.

### Permitted grid rupture

Break or distort the grid only when:

- a structure physically fails;
- Varek cannot fit the institutional frame;
- a concealed truth becomes undeniable;
- violence destroys routine;
- a character loses agency;
- the Mountain's scale must overwhelm the human frame.

If every page breaks the grid, the rupture has no meaning.

### Panel economy

Every panel must perform at least one job:

- advance action;
- reveal character;
- establish spatial information;
- introduce or reactivate evidence;
- change the reader's understanding;
- control time or silence.

Delete or merge panels that only repeat atmosphere.

### Wordless requirement

Every sequence should be tested without dialogue. If the core action becomes
incomprehensible, repair the staging before adding narration.

Use silent procedural sequences for:

- debt-band inspection;
- tool issue and return;
- respirator maintenance;
- ration division;
- shift transfer;
- body recovery;
- flame rites;
- survey work;
- machinery start-up and shutdown.

### Dialogue pressure

Dialogue is sparse, weary, specific, and grounded in work. Characters speak
about the immediate burden before the theme.

Prefer:

> “The second brace is split.”

over:

> “The Mountain's decaying institutions have failed us again.”

No generic prophecy, heroic banter, lore lecture, or modern therapy language.

## 8. Scene construction order

For every page or sequence, work in this order:

1. **Human need:** what does someone need before the scene ends?
2. **Institutional pressure:** who or what prevents it?
3. **Physical evidence:** what object or bodily mark proves the pressure?
4. **Spatial truth:** where are exits, hazards, supports, and people?
5. **Action:** what changes physically?
6. **Consequence:** what remains after the action?
7. **Page rhythm:** where does the reader pause, accelerate, or turn the page?
8. **Dialogue:** add only what the image cannot carry.

Do not begin with a cool camera angle or a finished render prompt.

## 9. Production workflow

### Gate 0 — Task classification

Declare whether the task is:

- script;
- thumbnail;
- panel specification;
- Blender implementation;
- render;
- page assembly;
- lettering;
- validation;
- human review preparation.

One task may produce several artifacts, but its authority must remain clear.

### Gate 1 — Canon preflight

- List the governing sources.
- List facts used by the scene.
- Mark every new invention as `PROPOSED_DESIGN`.
- Stop on unresolved canon conflict.

### Gate 2 — Visual beat sheet

For each panel declare:

- narrative function;
- focal subject;
- evidence token;
- dominant value mass;
- camera distance and angle;
- dialogue allocation;
- intended transition from the previous panel.

### Gate 3 — Spatial thumbnail

Verify:

- reading order;
- silhouette separation;
- balloon space;
- head and ground visibility where required;
- action geography;
- page-turn placement;
- thumbnail readability.

### Gate 4 — Runtime discovery

For Blender work, run the mandatory discovery preflight from
`docs/DETERMINISTIC_GRAPHIC_NOVEL_PIPELINE_PLAN.md`. Never guess runtime names
or capabilities.

### Gate 5 — Panel production

Use EEVEE as the graphic-novel panel master. Generate the required line, tone,
mask, halftone, and panel artifacts from the bound manifest.

### Gate 6 — Independent validation

The validator independently inspects artifacts from disk and emits a CARDO
claim. The producer cannot authorize its own output.

### Gate 7 — Story and art review

Run three separate reviews:

1. **Story:** does the page create a meaningful change?
2. **Visual:** does the page read at thumbnail size and preserve Thulan identity?
3. **Truth:** are all claims scoped, evidenced, and current?

### Gate 8 — Human approval

Aaron approves or rejects the exact page artifact by hash. Machine PASS does
not equal artistic approval.

## 10. Context and token economy

Token saving is subordinate to truth. Never remove governing evidence merely to
make a request cheaper. Reduce duplication, irrelevant context, speculative
calls, and oversized output first.

### Repository context discipline

For each task:

1. Search filenames and symbols before opening files.
2. Load the smallest authoritative set that can decide the task.
3. Read targeted sections when the whole document is not required by an active
   skill or repository instruction.
4. Refer to unchanged material by path, heading, line reference, revision, or
   hash instead of pasting it into every handoff.
5. Do not reread an unchanged file during the same task unless the required
   section was not captured or another process may have modified it.
6. Keep tool output scoped. Prefer matched lines, summaries, counts, diffs, and
   failing cases over full logs.
7. Preserve a short task ledger containing decisions, unresolved questions,
   inspected revisions, and the next smallest action.

Required reading order does not mean injecting every source into every model
call. Read the governing source, extract the binding facts, and carry those facts
forward with their provenance.

### Deterministic work before model work

Use deterministic code for:

- manifest and schema validation;
- file discovery and hashing;
- canon-key lookup;
- duplicate detection;
- image dimensions and channel inspection;
- Blender object and property discovery;
- arithmetic, threshold checks, and claim evaluation;
- unchanged-artifact detection.

Do not spend a model call rediscovering a fact that a parser, hash comparison,
test, or repository search can establish exactly. Event Forge Gate 0–5 filtering
must run before optional generative expansion when that path exists.

### Request economy

- Combine independent read-only lookups when the tooling safely supports it.
- Keep dependent steps ordered; do not ask several agents to rediscover the
  same state.
- Use one agent for short tasks, ordered chains, and edits to the same mutable
  files. Additional agents require a stated independent deliverable.
- Do not generate multiple prose variants unless selection between variants is
  part of the task.
- Bound requested output to the artifact actually needed. Do not ask for a full
  report when a status record or patch is sufficient.
- Reuse a verified result while its input hashes and governing revision remain
  unchanged. Invalidate it when either changes.

### Prompt-cache discipline for API orchestration

When CARDO or Event Forge makes repeated API requests:

- place stable instructions, schemas, examples, and reference material before
  task-specific content;
- keep tool definitions, tool ordering, model, reasoning configuration, and
  output schema stable across comparable requests;
- append changing task data after the reusable prefix;
- do not rewrite earlier conversation content merely for cosmetic consistency;
- use explicit cache breakpoints only when the selected model supports them and
  the stable prefix will be reused enough to justify cache-write cost;
- record cache reads and cache writes instead of assuming a session produced a
  cache hit.

Prompt caching lowers processing cost for a matching prefix. It does not reduce
the logical context presented to the model, guarantee identical output, or
replace validation.

### Long-run context control

For long agent runs, compact only after recording:

- governing source revisions;
- accepted decisions;
- rejected alternatives that must not return;
- artifact paths and hashes;
- checks executed and checks still outstanding;
- current blockers and next action.

After a valid compaction item or equivalent task ledger exists, discard obsolete
transcript detail rather than continually replaying it. Never compact away an
unresolved canon conflict or a failed check.

### Model and execution routing

- Use the smallest model and lowest reasoning effort that passes representative
  quality tests for the task class.
- Escalate only after a recorded failure, ambiguity, or risk threshold.
- Use asynchronous batch or lower-priority processing for non-interactive work
  only when turnaround requirements permit it.
- Reserve high-effort reasoning for canon conflicts, mechanical diagnosis,
  architecture, and final cross-domain review.

Model choice is an evaluated routing decision, not a permanent status symbol.

### Minimum usage record

Where the runtime exposes usage, record:

```yaml
usage:
  requests: 0
  input_tokens: 0
  cached_input_tokens: 0
  cache_write_tokens: 0
  output_tokens: 0
  reasoning_tokens: 0
  tool_calls: 0
  estimated_cost: null
  accepted_artifact: false
```

Judge efficiency by cost per accepted, validated artifact. A cheap request that
creates unusable work is not a saving.

## 11. Anti-hallucination law

Never invent:

- canon;
- object names;
- Blender state;
- render capability;
- file presence;
- measurements;
- evidence;
- dialogue approval;
- visual approval;
- test execution;
- successful output.

If required information is missing:

- use `BLOCKED` when an input or authority is absent;
- use `UNIMPLEMENTED` when the required executable capability does not exist;
- use `FAIL` when executed evidence contradicts the predicate;
- ask Aaron only when the missing choice materially changes canon, scope, or art
  direction.

Plausibility is not evidence. Detail is not authority. Confidence is not a
measurement.

## 13. Self-review questions

Before handing off any page, answer:

1. What does the human being in this page want?
2. What physically prevents it?
3. What evidence remains after the choice?
4. Can the page be understood without a lore briefing?
5. Can the core action be understood without dialogue?
6. Does the page still read at thumbnail size?
7. Is Varek visibly human rather than a robot?
8. Is the Mountain affecting behavior, or merely decorating the background?
9. Did the page earn every grid rupture and accent color?
10. Is any claim stronger than its evidence?
11. Did the agent invent a runtime fact or canon detail?
12. Was any context, request, or model escalation unnecessary?
13. What is the smallest honest next step?

Any unresolved answer blocks final handoff.

## 14. First proof-page work order

The initial page remains a three-panel institutional scene:

1. A records steward alters or hesitates over a death ledger under one amber
   lamp.
2. Varek enters the low room, stoops under the frame, and protects the steward's
   fragile work from his own mass.
3. A close physical record reveals that the official account is false: a survey
   slip predates the supposedly voluntary sacrifice, or a new lead strip charges
   the survivor for the rescue.

The page must prove:

- ordinary human stakes;
- Varek's scale without hero posing;
- institutional evidence;
- readable EEVEE ink and tone hierarchy;
- silent comprehension;
- reproducible panel artifacts;
- clear separation between concept, machine validation, and human approval.

If the page only proves that the renderer can make attractive machinery, it
fails the story gate.
