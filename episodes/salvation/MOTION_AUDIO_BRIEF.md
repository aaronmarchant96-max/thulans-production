# Salvation - motion and audio brief

## The people remain the centre

Ansen and Nessa bicker competitively and know each other's habits. Their humour comes from work, improvisation, and familiarity. When pain or fear becomes real, they become attentive and direct. Give the audience time to enjoy the relationship before asking them to grieve it.

The reunion allows a genuine gain. Nessa guides the fastening and chooses when to move. The first success helps explain Ansen's persistence. When she sees the handler returning, their immediate needs separate: he wants her free; she wants him clear.

## Image and motion

Use still images, selective layers, and modest motion. A static composition is allowed. Do not put a zoom on every panel or stretch the fatal action to display an effect.

Follow what the viewpoint character notices and needs. Background work, routes, clothing, shift sounds, and familiar habits should carry a sense of place. Avoid repeated threat inserts that announce the injury long before it happens.

Maintain the slit restriction through the rescue and open the view with mask removal. Match the page 11 and 12 crops and positions so that changed effort and results are legible. Distinguish Nessa's voluntary thumb from the gauntlet's imposed grip. Establish handler command and movement before the killing.

Page 14 has no flashback or slow-motion injury. Page 15 remains with Nessa long enough to understand her attempt to reach Ansen and the inspection record. Page 16 returns unmistakably to earlier intact fastenings and the prepared coat; it does not imply recovery or resurrection.

## Song and performance

The work-song is original and not yet composed. A close, unshowy voice and sparse accompaniment can preserve warmth while the image becomes violent. Meaningful words, breaths, pauses, and familiar gestures should be developed together. The three notes and wandering fourth are a draft motif, not a fixed pitch specification.

The Gears of War "Mad World" trailer is a reference for a gentle gesture toward a broken cultural object and the human vulnerability carried by restrained singing. This package neither supplies that recording nor claims verified word-to-frame timings. Do not copy its melody, lyrics, or cut timing into an original master by default.

Keep the gentle phrase through the fatal action as the current creative direction. Avoid an automatic horror sting or a triumphant swell. Leave space for the valve click, metal load, breath, and the ordinary work continuing nearby. Final balance depends on actual recordings.

## Voices and foley

- Childhood rivalry should sound habitual, including interruption and imperfect timing, rather than like prepared punchlines.
- Nessa's speech through the apparatus is constrained. Eyes, thumb, and timing share its meaning; do not give her unrestricted dialogue for convenience.
- Varek's few words cost breath. Preserve the author's pilot wording without speeding, pitch-shifting, or forcing it to fit a prototype master.
- The handler's ordinary actions and audible controls make responsibility visible. The degree of intent behind the lethal outcome is not settled by a neutral performance.
- Avoid monster roars, prolonged screaming for spectacle, and action-movie quips around the killing.

"Shift over" and "Let me see" remain possible relationship anchors. They are not newly approved recorded lines or mandatory death callbacks.
